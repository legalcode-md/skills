# Icelandic EEA Conformity Assessment Method

Read this reference before assigning a verdict. Use it with the controlling EEA act as
adapted by its Joint Committee Decision, not with an unadapted EU text alone.

## 1. Unit of assessment

Create one row per atomic legal proposition. Split compound paragraphs by addressee,
operator, beneficiary, trigger, exception, deadline, remedy, sanction, option, or reporting
duty. Keep definitions and recitals as linked interpretive context rather than free-standing
obligations unless the operative text makes them so.

## 2. Measure-sufficiency dispositions

Use a disposition in addition to the four track states:

- `EXPRESS_NIM`: a specific Icelandic provision implements the obligation.
- `MULTIPLE_NIMS`: the result emerges from several coordinated provisions.
- `EXISTING_BINDING_FRAMEWORK`: pre-existing general or sectoral law delivers the result.
- `BINDING_ADMINISTRATIVE_MEASURE`: an accessible, stable and enforceable administrative
  norm is legally sufficient for the obligation's character.
- `OPTION_NOT_EXERCISED`: Iceland permissibly declined a Member-State option.
- `NO_SPECIFIC_MEASURE`: the adapted obligation is institutional, relational, or otherwise
  requires no distinct national norm; explain why.
- `EXCLUDED_BY_ADAPTATION`: the JCD removes or modifies the obligation for Iceland.
- `NO_MAPPING`: no candidate implementation measure was found after documented retrieval.

Never turn a search failure into `NO_MAPPING` without a source-coverage analysis.

`NO_MAPPING` is an evidence disposition, not a transposition finding. Assign `NOT_TRANSPOSED`
only after the documented search and coverage analysis rule out a sufficient national measure.

## 3. Authority and act-family control

Freeze the controlling legal package for the target date before comparison. Include the base act,
amendments, corrigenda, recasts, repeals, delegated and implementing acts necessary to deliver the
required result, annexes, transitional provisions and binding standards incorporated by reference.
Apply the relevant EEA Annex or Protocol, JCD, adaptations and authentic incorporated text.

Classify each source as one of:

1. controlling EEA or Icelandic law;
2. binding judicial authority;
3. ESA decision or enforcement position;
4. homogeneity-relevant CJEU authority;
5. persuasive methodology; or
6. explanatory or factual material.

Do not permit a lower category to contradict or replace a higher category. Use Commission
conformity methodology as a persuasive process benchmark only, not as binding EEA law.

## 4. Four assessment tracks

### A. Notification and explanatory documents

Review every Form 1, including partial or corrective notifications, the ESA implementation-status
record, attached national measures and explanatory documents. Test whether Iceland communicated:

- every national measure and its full legal text;
- full title, English title or abbreviation, official link and effective date;
- a clear and precise link between each EEA provision and each national provision;
- an English translation where ESA reasonably needs one; and
- a reasoned explanation for existing-law reliance, non-exercise of an option, or no measure.

Use `NOT_NOTIFIED`, `PARTIALLY_NOTIFIED`, `NOTIFIED_INADEQUATE_EXPLANATION`, `FULLY_NOTIFIED`,
`NOT_APPLICABLE`, or `NOT_ASSESSABLE`. Notification is evidence of communication only; it does
not prove completeness or conformity.

### B. Transposition completeness

Check every article, sub-article, paragraph, point and annex obligation, including geographic and
substantive scope and mandatory derogations. Determine whether a legally sufficient measure exists
for every required result. A bare notification is incomplete if the measure or sufficiently precise
mapping is absent. Assign `EMPTY_SHELL` where national law only delegates or promises a future
methodology, institution, rule or procedure without presently delivering it.

Use `COMPLETE`, `PARTIAL_TRANSPOSITION`, `NOT_TRANSPOSED`, `EMPTY_SHELL`,
`NO_SPECIFIC_MEASURE_REQUIRED`, `NOT_APPLICABLE`, or `NOT_ASSESSABLE`.

### C. Substantive conformity

Use test A below as the applicability gate and test B on the transposition-completeness track.
Apply tests C–J to the combined legal effect of all sufficient national measures. Use
`CONFORMING`, `PARTIALLY_CONFORMING`, `INCORRECTLY_IMPLEMENTED`, `NOT_APPLICABLE`, or
`NOT_ASSESSABLE`. Do not import an application failure into this track unless it proves that the
national norm itself is legally insufficient or authoritatively interpreted incompatibly.

### D. Application and enforcement

Test reporting, action plans, facility creation, actual administration, supervision and enforcement
that fall due separately from legal transposition. Before assigning a defect, identify the actor and
classify the evidence as private conduct, isolated official error, authoritative interpretation, or
repeated/systemic State-attributable practice. Record frequency, consistency, duration, affected
class, supervisory response, final decisions and national remedies.

Use `NOT_ASSESSED`, `NO_BAD_APPLICATION_IDENTIFIED`, `ISOLATED_APPLICATION_INCIDENT`,
`SYSTEMIC_BAD_APPLICATION`, `NOT_APPLICABLE`, or `NOT_ASSESSABLE`. Never infer a systemic
finding from one unresolved complaint.

## 5. Ten correspondence tests

### A. Applicability and baseline

Confirm the act version and family, EEA incorporation, JCD adaptations, Annex or Protocol, entry
into force, deadline, target date, Article 103 notification and provisional-application status,
any Article 102 suspension, amendment chain, transitional provisions and territorial or sectoral
limitations. Failure here makes later comparison unreliable.

### B. Completeness

Check every atomic result: beneficiaries, addressees, acts and omissions, conditions,
exceptions, deadlines, procedures, reporting, supervision, remedies and sanctions. A mapping
is not complete merely because it cites the same subject matter.

### C. Personal and material scope

Compare definitions, thresholds, entity classes, products, services, activities, exclusions,
geography and cross-border facts. Test cross-references and incorporated definitions.
Do not classify a broader national definition as non-conforming solely because it is broader.
Identify whether the extension changes legal consequences, creates material uncertainty or
confusion, discriminates, or impedes an EEA right or market freedom.

### D. Operative content

Compare deontic force and conditions: must, may, may only, shall not, entitlement, discretion,
presumption, burden of proof, standard of review, and cumulative or alternative requirements.
Check whether the national context narrows or defeats copied words.
When Iceland exercises an option, derogation or discretion, test compliance with the secondary
act and applicable primary EEA rules, proportionality, equal treatment, fundamental rights and
free movement. For open norms, review authoritative judicial and administrative interpretations
and the consistency of their application.

### E. Clarity and precision

Ask whether regulated persons, beneficiaries, authorities and courts can identify the rule
and predict its application. Record ambiguity, conflicting provisions, undefined concepts,
open-ended delegation and misleading cross-references.

### F. Binding legal force

Identify the instrument's rank, promulgation, commencement, addressee and enforceability.
Policy, custom, internal practice, explanatory notes, ministerial statements and guidance are
not automatically binding implementation. State why a non-legislative measure is legally
sufficient if relying on it.

### G. Practical enforceability

Identify who can invoke or enforce the rule, against whom, before which authority, through
what process, within what deadline and with what interim/final remedy. Check costs, standing,
forms, evidence, review and appeal. Apply the national-procedural-autonomy requirements of
equivalence and effectiveness. A formally correct rule can be operationally ineffective.

### H. Remedies, sanctions and deterrence

Where the EEA act requires remedies or effective, proportionate and dissuasive sanctions,
test availability, coverage, ceiling/floor, limitation period, responsible authority,
procedural safeguards and actual capacity. Do not equate nominal availability with adequacy.

### I. Institutional and State duties

Test competent-authority designation, independence, powers, resources, cooperation, reporting
to ESA or other bodies, registries, information exchange and administrative organization.
Decide whether legislation, regulation, formal designation or an internal arrangement is
required based on the duty's legal nature and external effects.

### J. Temporal continuity and coherence

Check commencement, staggered application, savings, grandfathering, repeal, sunset, later
amendments and overlaps. Confirm there was no gap between the EEA deadline and Icelandic
effectiveness and no later amendment reopened the defect.

## 6. Decision rules

- Assign one state on each applicable track; never reduce the four tracks to one verdict.
- Assign `CONFORMING` only if all material substantive tests pass and the evidence chain is fetched.
- Assign `PARTIALLY_CONFORMING` when a missing element is severable and the remainder achieves part of the
  result. Name the missing element.
- Assign `INCORRECTLY_IMPLEMENTED` when a national rule positively alters or defeats the required result,
  even if the provision is mapped and most wording matches.
- Assign `NOT_TRANSPOSED` only after checking general law, regulations, later amendments,
  commencement, notified and unnotified candidate measures, and source-coverage gaps.
- Assign `EMPTY_SHELL` when a current measure postpones the required legal result to an unmade
  future instrument or action.
- Assign `NO_SPECIFIC_MEASURE_REQUIRED` only with a reasoned legal basis; never use it as a
  convenience category.
- Assign `NOT_ASSESSABLE` whenever missing evidence or a genuine interpretive conflict prevents a
  defensible result.

Operational equivalence requires proof of equivalent scope, beneficiaries, legal force,
enforcement, remedies, timing and practical result. A different form is permissible; a
different result is not.

## 7. EEA remedial analysis

Keep conformity and remedy analytically separate. For each defect ask:

1. Can Icelandic law be interpreted consistently without contradicting clear wording,
   legality, legal certainty or constitutional constraints?
2. Does Protocol 35 and its Icelandic implementation affect priority between the implemented
   EEA rule and another statutory rule?
3. Does verified EFTA Court authority support State liability or another remedy on these facts?
4. Can the claimant invoke the national implementing measure as written?
5. Does the proposed remedy help the affected person while leaving Iceland's implementation
   breach intact?
6. Do national procedural rules satisfy equivalence and effectiveness?

Do not label an EEA directive provision “directly effective” by copying the EU-pillar test.
Retrieve and cite the controlling EFTA Court and Icelandic authority for any proposition about
individual enforceability.

## 8. ESA evidence and procedure

For ESA-facing work, identify whether the matter is an information request, complaint screening,
letter of formal notice, reasoned opinion or possible Article 31 SCA referral. Record the response
deadline, State attribution, requested evidence, available national remedies and ESA's enforcement
discretion.

ESA must establish the alleged failure and cannot rely on a presumption alone. Iceland must
cooperate and substantively answer evidence sufficient to call compliance into question. Keep the
legal merits, evidential sufficiency and procedural posture in separate fields.

## 9. Aggregation

Report a separate transparent rate or tally for each track. If a conformity rate is useful:

`conformity_rate = CONFORMING / (CONFORMING + PARTIALLY_CONFORMING + INCORRECTLY_IMPLEMENTED)`

Do not put `NOT_TRANSPOSED`, `PARTIAL_TRANSPOSITION`, or `EMPTY_SHELL` into the conformity-rate
denominator; report them in the transposition track. Exclude `NOT_APPLICABLE` and
`NO_SPECIFIC_MEASURE_REQUIRED` only after human review. Never hide `NOT_ASSESSABLE`; report it
beside every rate and show every denominator.
