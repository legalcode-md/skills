## 1. Plain-language summary

_Plain-language: this section is a non-binding overview. The clauses below are the binding terms._

This MNDA is a two-way confidentiality agreement adapted for Icelandic law. Either Party may share or receive confidential information. The sharing Party is the Discloser (Upplýsingaveitandi); the receiving Party is the Recipient (Móttakandi).

Confidential Information (Trúnaðarupplýsingar) is anything non-public shared for the Purpose (Tilgangur) that a reasonable person would treat as confidential. Information that is already public, lawfully known to Recipient, lawfully obtained from a third party without restriction, or independently developed is excluded. Information meeting Article 2(1) of Directive (EU) 2016/943 as incorporated into the EEA legal order is given longer trade-secret protection.

Two clocks apply: the Disclosure Period and the Confidentiality Term. Trade secrets are protected for as long as they remain trade secrets. Nothing here prevents protected disclosures to Persónuvernd, Fjármálaeftirlitið ("FME"), Samkeppniseftirlitið, the police, the courts, or other competent authorities, or whistleblower reports under Icelandic law. Provisional injunctive relief (lögbann) and other equitable remedies are available alongside damages.

## 4. Confidential Information

_Plain-language: any non-public information shared for the Purpose is covered, whether or not labelled. Trade secrets are also covered._

Confidential Information means any non-public information one Party (as Discloser) makes available to the other (as Recipient) in connection with the Purpose, in any form, whether or not marked, that a reasonable person in the Recipient's position would understand to be confidential given the nature of the information and the circumstances of disclosure.

It includes, without limitation: technical information (designs, source code, algorithms, models, datasets, security architecture); business information (strategies, roadmaps, pricing, unannounced products); financial information; customer, supplier, and personnel information; Personal Data shared for the Purpose; and the existence and contents of the discussions.

Confidential Information may include information constituting a Trade Secret under the EEA-incorporated Trade Secrets Directive. Where it does, the additional remedies and longer term in Sections 9 and 13 apply on top of the general obligations.

---

## 5. Permitted disclosures & use

_Plain-language: Recipient may use the information only for the Purpose, and may share it only with people who actually need to see it._

Recipient shall use Discloser's Confidential Information solely for the Purpose, and shall not use it to compete with Discloser, reverse-engineer Discloser's products, or develop competing products, except as expressly permitted here or required by mandatory law.

Recipient may disclose Confidential Information only to Representatives (Umboðsaðilar) who need to know it for the Purpose and are bound by written confidentiality obligations no less protective than those here. Icelandic-licensed lawyers (lögmenn under Lög nr. 77/1998) and other professional advisers subject to professional duties of equivalent stringency satisfy this requirement.

Recipient is responsible for any act or omission of its Representatives that would constitute a breach if undertaken by Recipient. Recipient shall promptly notify Discloser of any actual or suspected unauthorised use or disclosure and cooperate in mitigation, including any notification obligation under Act 90/2018 or the GDPR.

---

## 6. Standard of care

_Plain-language: protect the other side's information at least as carefully as you protect your own — and never less than reasonably._

Recipient shall protect Confidential Information using at least the same degree of care that it uses for its own confidential information of similar sensitivity, and in any event no less than a reasonable standard. This includes administrative, technical, and physical safeguards appropriate to the sensitivity of the information.

Where the information includes a Trade Secret, the steps taken shall be capable of qualifying as "reasonable steps under the circumstances to keep it secret" within Article 2(1)(c) of Directive (EU) 2016/943 as incorporated via the EEA. Where it includes Personal Data, Recipient shall apply security measures consistent with Article 32 of the GDPR.

---

## 7. Exclusions

_Plain-language: information that is already public, already known, independently developed, or lawfully obtained from a third party is not protected. Whistleblowing and supervisory-authority disclosures are not restrained._

The obligations in this MNDA do not apply to information that Recipient can demonstrate by contemporaneous records:

- (a) was already in Recipient's possession, without obligation of confidentiality, before being received from Discloser;
- (b) is or becomes generally available to the public through no act or omission of Recipient or its Representatives in breach of this MNDA;
- (c) was lawfully received by Recipient from a third party without obligation of confidentiality and without breach by that third party of any duty owed to Discloser; or
- (d) was independently developed by or for Recipient without reference to Discloser's Confidential Information.

In addition, this MNDA shall not restrain:

- (e) any disclosure or use covered by the Article 5 carve-outs of Directive (EU) 2016/943 as incorporated via the EEA `[VERIFY EEA incorporation reference]`, including freedom of expression and information, whistleblowing in the public interest, and worker representation;
- (f) any protected disclosure made under Lög nr. 40/2020 um vernd uppljóstrara (the Icelandic Whistleblower Protection Act) `[VERIFY current short title and Act number]`; any provision purporting to restrain such a disclosure is, to that extent, of no effect;
- (g) any disclosure required to give effect to a data subject's right of access under Article 17 of Act 90/2018 and Article 15 of the GDPR, or any other data-subject right under Articles 16 to 22 of the GDPR; and
- (h) any disclosure to Persónuvernd, Fjármálaeftirlitið ("FME"), Samkeppniseftirlitið, the EFTA Surveillance Authority, Ríkisendurskoðun, or any other Icelandic or EEA-level supervisory or regulatory authority acting within its statutory mandate.

These exclusions do not, by themselves, give Recipient any right to publish or commercially exploit information that may also constitute Personal Data, regulated data, or a Trade Secret; obligations under such law continue to apply on their own terms.

## 8. Compelled disclosure

_Plain-language: if a court or regulator forces disclosure, give the other side notice (where allowed) and only hand over what the law actually requires._

If Recipient becomes legally compelled — by an order of an Icelandic court (Héraðsdómur, Landsréttur, Hæstiréttur Íslands), a request from Persónuvernd, FME, Samkeppniseftirlitið, Skatturinn, Lögreglan, Ríkissaksóknari, an EEA supervisory authority, a parliamentary inquiry under Lög nr. 55/1991 um þingsköp Alþingis, a request under Lög nr. 140/2012 um upplýsingar (the Icelandic FOIA-equivalent), or any other competent authority — to disclose any Confidential Information, Recipient shall, where legally permitted, give Discloser prompt written notice sufficient to allow Discloser to seek a protective order or other relief.

Recipient shall reasonably cooperate, at Discloser's expense, with any such effort, including supporting an application for confidential treatment under Article 9 of Directive (EU) 2016/943 (as incorporated via the EEA) for documents containing a Trade Secret. If a protective order is not obtained, Recipient shall disclose only the portion legally required, shall use commercially reasonable efforts to obtain assurance of confidential treatment, and shall continue to treat the remainder as Confidential Information.

Nothing here prevents a good-faith report of a suspected violation of law to a competent authority or a protected whistleblower disclosure under Lög nr. 40/2020 `[VERIFY]`. Notice to Discloser is not required where giving notice would itself be unlawful, would compromise an investigation, or would breach the whistleblower protections in Section 7(f).

## 9. Term & duration of obligations

_Plain-language: there are three clocks — how long you can share, how long protection lasts after sharing, and a separate clock for trade secrets. Excessive duration may be set aside under Lög nr. 7/1936 § 36._

This MNDA takes effect on the Effective Date and continues until terminated under this Section.

The Parties may exchange Confidential Information during the Disclosure Period of {{Disclosure Period}} (default: tvö (2) ár / two (2) years from the Effective Date). Either Party may terminate the Disclosure Period at any time on written notice.

Recipient's obligations of confidentiality and non-use with respect to each item continue for the Confidentiality Term of {{Confidentiality Term}} (default: fimm (5) ár / five (5) years from disclosure of that item).

Notwithstanding the Confidentiality Term, obligations with respect to a Trade Secret continue for {{Trade-Secret Term}} (default: for so long as the information remains a trade secret within Article 2(1) of Directive (EU) 2016/943 as incorporated via the EEA). Where a longer minimum period is required by Icelandic or EEA law (for example, retained Personal Data, FME-supervised data, or specific Trade Secrets), that longer period applies.

**Note on Lög nr. 7/1936 § 36.** Under Lög nr. 7/1936 um samningsgerð, umboð og ógilda löggerninga § 36 — the Icelandic invalidity provision equivalent to the Nordic § 36 — a contractual term that is unreasonable (ósanngjarn) or contrary to good business practice may be modified or set aside. Excessively long or one-sided confidentiality obligations may be vulnerable in B2C, employment-adjacent, or asymmetric-bargaining contexts; consider shortening the default Confidentiality Term in such cases.

Termination does not relieve Recipient of accrued obligations or those that by their nature survive (Sections 5 to 13).

## 10. Return or destruction of materials

_Plain-language: when asked, give it back or delete it — with sensible carve-outs for backups and legal copies._

On Discloser's written request, or on expiration of the Disclosure Period, Recipient shall, at Discloser's option, return or destroy all Confidential Information of Discloser in the possession of Recipient or its Representatives, including copies, extracts, and derivatives, within {{Return Window}} (default: thirty (30) days / þrjátíu (30) dagar). On request, Recipient shall provide written certification of destruction.

Recipient is not required to delete or destroy:

- (a) copies retained automatically in standard backup, archival, disaster-recovery, or business-continuity systems, provided they are not accessed for any operational purpose and are deleted in the ordinary course;
- (b) one (1) copy retained by Recipient's Icelandic legal counsel, or by a Representative subject to a legal-hold or regulatory record-keeping obligation (including under Lög nr. 145/1994 um bókhald, FME guidance, or Skatturinn requirements); and
- (c) information stored solely in the unaided memory of authorised Representatives.

Information retained under this Section remains subject to this MNDA for as long as it is retained. Personal Data so retained must additionally be processed in accordance with Act 90/2018 and the GDPR.

## 11. No license; ownership

_Plain-language: receiving information is not the same as getting a licence — Discloser keeps all of its IP._

As between the Parties, all Confidential Information remains the property of Discloser. Nothing here grants, by implication, estoppel, or otherwise, any right or licence under any patent, copyright, trade-mark, registered design, database right, trade secret, or other intellectual-property right of Discloser, except the limited right to use Confidential Information solely for the Purpose during the Disclosure Period.

No Party is obligated to disclose any particular information, to enter any further agreement, or to refrain from agreements with third parties (including competitors of the other Party), subject only to the express obligations here. Each Party acknowledges that the other may develop competing products or services; this MNDA does not restrict either Party's general business activities except as expressly stated.

## 13. Remedies & equitable relief

_Plain-language: damages alone may not be enough — courts can order the breaching party to stop, including via lögbann._

Each Party acknowledges that breach may cause irreparable harm for which monetary damages alone are inadequate. In addition to any other remedies, Discloser may seek:

- (a) provisional injunctive relief in the form of lögbann under Lög nr. 31/1990 um kyrrsetningu, lögbann o.fl., to prevent or restrain a threatened or actual breach;
- (b) damages under contract law and, where applicable, under Icelandic tort law (skaðabótaréttur), including direct loss and lost profits;
- (c) the substantive remedies prescribed by Articles 10 to 14 of Directive (EU) 2016/943 as incorporated into the EEA legal order `[VERIFY transposition mechanism in Icelandic law]`, including cessation of unlawful use, corrective measures (recall, destruction, removal from market), an account of unfair profits, compensation for actual loss, and where appropriate compensation for moral prejudice (miska);
- (d) an account of profits under general principles of Icelandic unjust enrichment (auðgunarréttur); and
- (e) any other remedy available under Icelandic law or in equity.

Remedies are cumulative and not exclusive; no election of one remedy excludes another. The unconscionability set-aside in Lög nr. 7/1936 § 36 is intended to protect parties from unreasonable terms; it is not available as a defence to a Recipient that has profited from unauthorised use or disclosure of Discloser's Confidential Information and now seeks release from its obligations.

## 16. Miscellaneous

_Plain-language: standard housekeeping clauses, plus a mandatory whistleblower-protection notice required under Icelandic public-policy principles._

**Entire agreement.** This MNDA, with Annex A, Annex B, and any signed jurisdictional supplement, constitutes the entire agreement between the Parties on its subject matter and supersedes all prior understandings on that subject matter.

**No waiver.** A failure or delay in exercising any right does not waive it. A waiver is effective only if in writing and signed by the waiving Party, and applies only to the specific instance.

**Severability.** If any provision is held invalid, illegal, or unenforceable by an Icelandic court or under Lög nr. 7/1936 § 36, that provision shall be modified to the minimum extent necessary to make it enforceable while preserving the Parties' original intent, and the remainder shall continue in full force.

**Assignment.** Neither Party may assign this MNDA without the other's prior written consent, except that either Party may assign without consent to a successor in a merger (samruni), acquisition, corporate reorganisation, or sale of all or substantially all of its assets relating to the Purpose, provided the successor agrees in writing to be bound and provides its kennitala. Any assignment in breach is void.

**Independent contractors.** The Parties are independent contracting parties (sjálfstæðir samningsaðilar). This MNDA does not create any partnership, joint venture, agency, fiduciary, or employment relationship.

**No publicity.** Neither Party shall use the other's name, logo, or trade marks, or disclose the existence or contents of the discussions or this MNDA, in any public statement or marketing material, without the other's prior written consent, except as required by Section 8 or by mandatory Icelandic disclosure law (including FME listing rules where applicable).

**Kennitala identification.** Each Party's kennitala stated in the preamble and Annex A is the controlling identifier for this MNDA. A change of legal name without a change of kennitala does not affect the identity of the Party; a change of kennitala (for example on corporate reorganisation) shall be promptly notified under Section 14 with documentary evidence (a vottorð from Skatturinn or Fyrirtækjaskrá).

**Counterparts; electronic signatures.** This MNDA may be signed in counterparts, each an original. The Parties consent to execution by electronic signature compliant with eIDAS as incorporated via the EEA, and Lög nr. 28/2001 um rafrænar undirskriftir. An electronic or scanned signature has the same effect as an original handwritten signature.

**Headings.** Headings are for convenience only and do not affect interpretation.

**Yfirlýsing um vernd uppljóstrara / Whistleblower-protection notice.**

> **Yfirlýsing um vernd uppljóstrara:** Þetta samkomulag hindrar ekki að aðili eða umboðsmenn hans tilkynni um ætluð brot á lögum, lagaákvæðum eða reglum til þar til bærra eftirlitsstofnana, lögreglu eða dómstóla, í samræmi við lög um vernd uppljóstrara nr. 40/2020 og önnur viðeigandi lög. Ákvæði sem ætlað er að hindra slíkar tilkynningar eru ógild að því marki.

> **Whistleblower-protection notice (English equivalent):** This Agreement does not prevent any Party or its Representatives from reporting suspected breaches of law, regulation, or rules to a competent supervisory authority, the police, or the courts, in accordance with Lög nr. 40/2020 um vernd uppljóstrara `[VERIFY]` and other applicable law. Any provision purporting to restrain such reporting is void to that extent.

Defaults reflect a conservative reading of common Icelandic market practice but may not be appropriate for every transaction.
