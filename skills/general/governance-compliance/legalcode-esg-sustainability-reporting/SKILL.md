## Purpose and Scope

This skill assesses ESG and sustainability reporting obligations, identifies compliance gaps,
guides double and single materiality assessments, supports GHG emissions scoping, evaluates
greenwashing risk, and produces actionable remediation roadmaps for organizations subject to
mandatory or voluntary sustainability disclosure.

**Covers:**

- Framework applicability screening (CSRD, CSDDD, UK SDR/SRS, SEC, California SB 253/261, ISSB)
- Double materiality assessment (impact materiality + financial materiality) per ESRS 1
- Single/financial materiality assessment (ISSB S1/S2, TCFD)
- GHG emissions inventory review (Scope 1, Scope 2, Scope 3 — all 15 categories)
- ESRS topical standard analysis (E1–E5, S1–S4, G1)
- EU Taxonomy alignment, eligibility, and KPI disclosure
- Supply chain due diligence analysis (CSDDD, LkSG, France Loi de Vigilance, UK MSA)
- Greenwashing risk assessment (EU Directive 2024/825, UK CMA/FCA, US FTC)
- Assurance readiness (limited assurance per ISSA 5000, CSRD Article 26a requirements)
- Net-zero and carbon-neutrality claim verification
- Gap classification, severity scoring, and remediation roadmap

**Does not:**

- Draft a full sustainability report (the output is a gap assessment and action plan, not a
  finished report)
- Provide financial accounting advice or replace a statutory auditor
- Provide legal advice or replace qualified ESG/sustainability counsel
- Apply to one jurisdiction exclusively — multi-jurisdictional with regime-specific markers

## Workflow

### Step 1: Accept Input

Accept input in any of these formats:

- **Existing disclosures**: Prior sustainability reports, TCFD reports, CDP submissions,
  integrated reports with ESG sections, NFRD/NFI statements
- **Draft documents**: Draft CSRD sustainability statement, draft TCFD/SRS report, draft
  net-zero or carbon-neutrality claim, draft SEC climate disclosure
- **Program materials**: Materiality assessment reports, GHG inventory methodology
  documents, internal carbon accounting procedures, supply chain due diligence policies
- **Organizational data**: GHG emissions datasets (Scope 1/2/3), energy consumption
  records, social KPI data, Taxonomy KPI calculations
- **Regulatory inquiry**: EU regulator information request, SEC comment letter, investor
  ESG questionnaire, rating agency data request
- **Verbal description**: Organization type, sector, jurisdiction, revenue, employee count,
  current reporting state, specific question or gap to address

If no materials are provided, proceed to Step 2 — the framework applicability and gap
analysis can be produced based on contextual answers alone.

### Step 2: Gather Context

**⟁ CLARIFY** — Ask these questions before beginning substantive analysis. Present
structured options. Skip any question the user has already answered.

**Q1 — Operating Mode**

What is the primary objective?

- **Build**: Establish an ESG reporting program or CSRD sustainability statement from
  scratch (produces: reporting blueprint, data collection architecture, materiality
  assessment framework, assurance readiness plan)
- **Assess**: Evaluate existing disclosures or a draft report against applicable framework
  requirements (produces: gap assessment scorecard, severity-classified findings, remediation
  roadmap)
- **Remediate**: Address specific identified gaps — a prior audit finding, a regulator
  query, or a known greenwashing exposure (produces: targeted remediation plan for
  identified issues)
- **Claim Review**: Assess a specific net-zero, carbon-neutral, or ESG marketing claim
  for accuracy and greenwashing risk (produces: claim-specific risk analysis and hardening
  recommendations)
- **Scope 3 Scoping**: Map and quantify Scope 3 value chain emissions for the first time
  (produces: category-by-category materiality and data-sourcing plan)
- _Why this matters_: The deliverable format, analytical depth, and regulatory focus differ
  materially across modes.

**Q2 — Organization Profile**

Which profile best describes the organization?

- **Large EU undertaking**: Meets CSRD large company criteria (two of three: ≥250 employees,
  ≥€40M net turnover, ≥€20M total assets); or EU-listed company — CSRD mandatory; refer to
  Step 3 for phasing
- **Non-EU parent with EU nexus**: Non-EU company with EU-listed securities or substantial
  EU subsidiary; Article 40a CSRD extension may apply [VERIFY current Art. 40a implementation
  status]; EU net turnover >€450M triggers Wave 4 (FY 2028 reporting, FY 2027 for post-
  Omnibus large EU subsidiaries)
- **UK company**: UK-incorporated; SECR mandatory if large; FCA climate disclosure if
  listed; UK SRS S1/S2 proposed mandatory from January 2027 for listed companies (FCA CP26/5)
- **US public company**: SEC registrant; SEC climate rules stayed [VERIFY]; California
  SB 253 if revenue >$1B doing business in CA; California SB 261 if revenue >$500M [VERIFY
  injunction status]
- **Multinational — EU/UK/US**: Multiple concurrent obligations; produce multi-regime
  comparison
- **Private company — investor/voluntary only**: No mandatory obligations; ISSB/GRI/CDP
  voluntary adoption; investor or lender ESG due diligence requirements
- _Why this matters_: Determines which obligations are mandatory vs. voluntary, applicable
  timelines, and assurance requirements.

**Q3 — Primary Sector**

What is the organization's primary sector?

- **Energy, utilities, oil and gas**: ESRS E1 climate presumptively material; Scope 3
  Category 11 (use of sold products) material; ESRS E2/E3 likely material; Climate
  Delegated Act Taxonomy TSC apply
- **Financial services**: Financed emissions (Scope 3 Category 15); TCFD/PCAF standards;
  SFDR overlap for asset managers; Green Asset Ratio for banks; ECB climate stress testing
- **Agriculture, food, land use**: ESRS E4 biodiversity likely material; TNFD; Scope 3
  Category 1 (purchased goods) and Category 11 dominant
- **Technology and services**: Scope 3 Category 11 (AI/data center energy use) potentially
  material; Scope 1/2 typically manageable; supply chain Scope 3 Categories 1/2
- **Retail and consumer goods**: Scope 3 Categories 1 and 11 dominant; supply chain human
  rights (ESRS S2/CSDDD); packaging/waste (ESRS E5)
- **Heavy manufacturing, chemicals, cement, steel**: ESRS E1/E2 material; process
  emissions; Taxonomy EU-eligible activities; SBTi Hard-to-Abate pathways
- **Construction and real estate**: Use-phase Scope 3 Category 11; ESRS E5 circular
  economy; embodied carbon; CRREM analysis
- **Healthcare and pharma**: Supply chain API sourcing (ESRS S2); Scope 3 Category 1;
  medical waste (ESRS E2/E5)
- _Why this matters_: Sector determines which ESRS topical standards are presumptively
  material and which Scope 3 categories require deepest analysis.

**Q4 — Current Reporting Maturity**

Which best describes the current ESG reporting state?

- **None**: No formal sustainability reporting; first-year assessment
- **Voluntary/narrative only**: Annual CSR narrative; no audited data; no framework alignment
- **CDP/GRI/TCFD responding**: Data-driven voluntary reporting; some assurance; partial
  framework compliance
- **Preparing for CSRD (first year)**: Undertaking gap assessment and implementing
  CSRD-compliant processes ahead of first mandatory filing
- **CSRD Year 1 (recently filed)**: First CSRD sustainability statement filed; seeking
  post-filing gap analysis
- **Established mandatory reporter**: Multi-year mandatory reporter (NFRD/NFI previously;
  now CSRD Wave 1); seeking enhancement, assurance readiness, or Omnibus I impact analysis
- _Why this matters_: Determines the depth of remediation required and whether the focus
  is foundational infrastructure or refinement.

**Q5 — Materiality Standard Required**

Which materiality approach is relevant?

- **Double materiality** (EU CSRD/ESRS): Both impact materiality (organization's effects
  on people/environment through its own operations and value chain) AND financial materiality
  (how sustainability matters affect financial performance, position, and cash flows); a
  topic is material if it meets either threshold
- **Single materiality — financial only** (ISSB S1/S2, TCFD, SEC): Only investor-focused
  financial materiality — how sustainability issues affect enterprise value
- **Both** (multinational with EU and investor-grade reporting): Document which topics are
  material under each lens; explain differences
- **Not yet assessed**: Proceed to Step 4 for materiality assessment guidance before other
  steps
- _Why this matters_: Double vs. single materiality produces different topic sets, different
  data requirements, and different assurance obligations.

**Q6 — Assurance Requirement**

What assurance is required or planned?

- **Limited assurance** (CSRD mandatory minimum): Negative-assurance standard; ISSA 5000
  application; "nothing has come to our attention"; Article 26a of CSRD; CEAOB limited
  assurance guidelines (September 2024)
- **Reasonable assurance** (CSRD progressive pathway — now removed by Omnibus I; investor
  requirement): Positive-assurance standard; significantly higher data quality and controls
  requirements
- **No external assurance**: Voluntary reporting; private company; internal review only
- **Assurance readiness assessment**: Include an assurance gap analysis as a deliverable
- _Why this matters_: Assurance requirements dictate data quality, internal controls, audit
  trail, and source documentation the organization must maintain.

### Step 4: Double Materiality Assessment Guidance

For organizations undertaking their first CSRD materiality assessment, or reviewing an
existing DMA, apply the following structured approach per ESRS 1.

**⟁ CLARIFY** — Ask how far the DMA process has progressed:

- **Not started**: Walk through the full DMA methodology
- **Underway**: Review the current approach for ESRS 1 compliance and identify gaps
- **Completed**: Validate whether the completed DMA would satisfy CSRD requirements and
  assurance scrutiny
- **Review only (not CSRD)**: Single-materiality (ISSB/TCFD financial-only) assessment

#### Double Materiality — Two Lenses per ESRS 1

**Lens 1 — Impact Materiality (Inside-Out)**

Assess the organization's actual and potential impacts on people and the environment through:

- Own operations
- Upstream value chain (suppliers, raw material extraction)
- Downstream value chain (product use, end-of-life)

For each sustainability matter in the ESRS standards, assess:

| Criterion           | Negative Impacts                           | Positive Impacts |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ---------------- |
| **Scale**           | How widespread globally or regionally?     | How substantial? |
| **Scope**           | How many people/ecosystems affected?       | —                |
| **Irremediability** | How reversible? (key for negative impacts) | —                |
| **Likelihood**      | How probable? (for potential impacts)      | How probable?    |

A matter is impact-material if the composite assessment crosses a materiality threshold the
company sets (must be documented and defensible to assurance providers).

**Lens 2 — Financial Materiality (Outside-In)**

Assess which sustainability matters generate material financial risks and opportunities
affecting the organization's:

- Cash flows (near, medium, long term)
- Access to and cost of financing
- Cost of capital
- Revenue trajectory and asset values

Use risk taxonomy aligned with TCFD: physical risks (acute: extreme weather events;
chronic: sea-level rise, temperature shift) and transition risks (policy, legal, technology,
market, reputation).

Opportunity categories: resource efficiency, energy sources, products/services, markets,
resilience.

A matter is financially material if it meets the IASB materiality definition: information
is material if its omission, misstatement, or obscuration could reasonably influence
decisions made by the primary users of the financial statements.

**Omnibus I Amended DMA Approach (from FY 2027):**

- Optional top-down approach (starting from business model/sector risks) alongside the
  existing bottom-up approach
- "Information Materiality Filter" — only IROs expected to generate material disclosures
  need deep analysis
- "Reasonable and proportionate evidence" standard
- Reduced stakeholder engagement documentation obligations

### Step 5: GHG Emissions Inventory Analysis

Assess the quality, completeness, and framework-compliance of the organization's GHG
emissions inventory.

**⟁ CLARIFY** — Which inventory elements need analysis? (Select all that apply)

- Scope 1 methodology and boundary
- Scope 2 methodology (market-based vs. location-based)
- Scope 3 — which categories are in scope and completeness of quantification
- Base year selection and intensity metrics
- GHG Protocol alignment and calculation quality
- SBTi target coverage and boundary

#### Scope 1 Analysis

Verify:

- **Organizational boundary method**: Equity share, financial control, or operational
  control. Document basis and apply consistently.
- **GHG coverage**: CO2, CH4, N2O, HFCs, PFCs, SF6, NF3 (as applicable to operations)
- **Emission sources**: Stationary combustion (boilers, furnaces), mobile combustion
  (owned fleet), process emissions (industrial chemical reactions, cement calcination),
  fugitive emissions (refrigerants, methane leaks, coal mine methane)
- **Emission factors**: Jurisdiction-specific IPCC/DEFRA/EPA factors; latest GWP values
  from IPCC AR6 (GWP100: CH4 = 27.9, N2O = 273) [VERIFY against organization's reporting
  period requirements — IFRS S2 December 2025 amendments permit jurisdictional relief
  from IPCC GWP requirement]
- **Verification**: Meter readings, fuel purchase records, process data linked to calculation

#### Scope 2 Analysis

For CSRD/ESRS E1 and IFRS S2: **both market-based and location-based methods required**.

| Method             | Approach                                                                          | Issues to Check                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Location-based** | Average grid emission factor for the country/region where electricity is consumed | Use IEA or national grid factors; check vintage (should be ≤2 years old)                                                              |
| **Market-based**   | Emission factor from contractual instruments (PPAs, RECs, GOs, GreenE)            | Verify GHG Protocol Scope 2 Quality Criteria: additionality, geography, temporality, vintage; check if RECs are bundled vs. unbundled |

**Market-based Alert:** Purchasing unbundled RECs from a grid with high renewable penetration
may satisfy Scope 2 market-based accounting but does not represent additional decarbonization.
SBTi's latest criteria require that PPAs/GOs meet geographic and temporal matching requirements
for target boundary compliance.

| Category | Description                              | Typical Materiality                            | Key Data Sources                                       |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1        | Purchased goods and services             | HIGH for most sectors                          | Supplier primary data; spend-based or hybrid methods   |
| 2        | Capital goods                            | MEDIUM                                         | Spend-based; equipment lifecycle data                  |
| 3        | Fuel- and energy-related (not Scope 1/2) | MEDIUM                                         | Upstream fuel extraction and transmission factors      |
| 4        | Upstream transport and distribution      | MEDIUM                                         | Freight data; 3PL records                              |
| 5        | Waste generated in operations            | LOW-MEDIUM                                     | Waste tonnage and treatment method                     |
| 6        | Business travel                          | LOW-MEDIUM                                     | Expense data; distance-based                           |
| 7        | Employee commuting                       | LOW                                            | Commute survey; location-based                         |
| 8        | Upstream leased assets                   | MEDIUM (if large real estate)                  | Landlord energy data; metered consumption              |
| 9        | Downstream transport and distribution    | MEDIUM (if sold products)                      | Logistics partner data                                 |
| 10       | Processing of sold intermediate products | LOW-MEDIUM                                     | Downstream processor data                              |
| 11       | Use of sold products                     | **HIGH** (energy, automotive, tech, chemicals) | Product energy/fuel consumption in use phase           |
| 12       | End-of-life treatment                    | LOW-MEDIUM                                     | Product mass; waste treatment mix                      |

**Scope 3 under IFRS S2 (December 2025 Amendment):** Category 15 (investments) now permits

— a significant simplification for financial sector entities.

**SBTi Coverage Requirement:** SBTi V5.3 requires Scope 3 targeting if Scope 3 ≥40% of
total Scope 1+2+3. When required, ≥67% of total Scope 3 must be covered across one or more

## ESG Compliance Deep Analysis Topics

### Topic 1: CSRD Scope Assessment

**Depth: Deep**

Key review points:

- Apply post-Omnibus I employee and turnover thresholds; confirm both cumulative conditions met (not just one)
- Verify which reporting wave the organization falls into and first mandatory financial year
- Assess whether any "stop-the-clock" transitional exemptions apply [VERIFY Omnibus I transitional provisions]
- Confirm whether the company is a PIEE (public-interest entity) — different thresholds than large companies
- Identify any subsidiary reporting scenarios: subsidiaries can be consolidated into group CSRD sustainability statement; must document consolidation approach

### Topic 2: ESRS 2 — General Disclosures Compliance

**Depth: Deep**

Key review points:

- **GOV-1 through GOV-5**: Board oversight of sustainability — board composition, expertise, roles; sustainability-related governance committee structure; executive pay linkage to sustainability targets
- **SBP-1 through SBP-4**: Strategy and business model — how sustainability matters are integrated into the overall business model; value chain mapping; stakeholder engagement
- **IRO-1**: Materiality assessment process — documented DMA methodology; stakeholder engagement records; engagement frequency
- **IRO-2**: List of all material IROs with disclosure requirements applied to each
- All ESRS 2 disclosures are **mandatory — not subject to the materiality filter** — flag if any are absent

### Topic 3: Climate Transition Plan (ESRS E1-1)

**Depth: Deep**

Key review points:

- Does the transition plan include explicit milestones for emissions reductions at regular intervals to 2050?
- Is the plan Paris Agreement-aligned (1.5°C pathway)?
- Does the plan include CapEx and OpEx funding commitments and timelines?
- Does the plan address fossil fuel phaseout / just transition considerations?
- Is the plan approved by the board and linked to executive compensation?
- Has the plan been validated by SBTi or equivalent independent body?
- Does the plan address technology readiness and key dependencies (e.g., green hydrogen, CCUS)?

### Topic 4: Physical and Transition Climate Risk Scenario Analysis

**Depth: Deep**

Key review points:

- Has scenario analysis been conducted? What scenarios were used?
- Are at least two contrasting scenarios used — one consistent with limiting warming to 1.5°C and one consistent with higher warming (≥3°C)? [Best practice: 1.5°C, 2°C, 4°C bracketing]
- Are physical risks assessed at asset/facility level (not just company level)?
- Are time horizons defined as short (0-3 years), medium (3-10 years), and long term (>10 years)?
- Are financial impacts of risks quantified or at least qualitatively characterized?
- Does scenario analysis feed into strategy and financial planning?

### Topic 5: Scope 3 Value Chain Emissions

**Depth: Deep** (See Step 5 for detailed category analysis)

- Are immaterial categories documented with the rationale for exclusion?
- Is biogenic carbon accounted for separately?

**Depth: Standard**

## Severity Classification System

### Full Classification Guide

#### 🟢 COMPLIANT

- The disclosure meets applicable requirements
- Methodology is documented and consistent with GHG Protocol or applicable ESRS standard
- Data is verifiable with adequate source documentation
- No material deficiencies identified

_Action_: Record finding; identify any best-practice enhancement opportunities.

#### 🟡 PARTIAL

- The disclosure addresses the requirement but with identified gaps
- Examples: correct methodology applied but missing one or more material categories; disclosure
  present but not quantified; ESRS required data points present but without comparative data;
  DMA conducted but stakeholder engagement not documented
- No immediate legal risk but creates assurance challenge or regulatory gap

_Action_: Remediate before next reporting cycle; provide specific remediation steps.

#### 🔴 NON-COMPLIANT

- The requirement is not met
- Examples: mandatory ESRS 2 disclosure absent; Scope 1/2 emissions not reported despite SECR
  applying; CSRD sustainability statement materially deficient; Taxonomy KPIs not disclosed
  despite CSRD applicability; due diligence policy absent despite LkSG or CSDDD applicability
- Significant regulatory and reputational risk

_Action_: Immediate remediation required; escalate to board/senior management; legal review
recommended for enforcement-risk items.

#### ⚫ CRITICAL / URGENT

- Severe legal exposure; enforcement action risk is plausible
- Examples: published greenwashing claim violating Directive 2024/825; net-zero claim
  unsupported by any transition plan; CSRD filing deadline missed with no plan; SEC climate
  disclosure incorrect and unretracted; CSDDD enforcement in progress; litigation commenced
  over ESG statements

_Action_: Immediate escalation; qualified legal counsel involvement strongly recommended;
consider whether disclosure should be retracted or corrected immediately.

**Pass 2 — Completeness**

- Have all applicable national implementing laws been considered (not just the EU directive)?
- Are there sector-specific exemptions or derogations?
- Has the organization's specific facts been correctly matched to the regulatory threshold?

**Pass 3 — Challenge**

- What is the strongest argument that this is not an urgent risk?
- Under what circumstances would a competent ESG counsel conclude this is manageable?
- Is there pending regulatory guidance or a consultation that would change the analysis?

## Anti-Patterns for ESG Reporting

These are the most common — and most legally dangerous — mistakes organizations make. Do
not let any of the following appear in outputs or recommendations.

1. **"Carbon neutral" via offsets only without emissions reduction**: Purchasing offsets to
   claim carbon neutrality without a documented reduction pathway violates EU Directive
   2024/825 and is the most common enforcement target. Claims must be reduction-first;
   offsets cover only verified residual emissions after maximum reduction.

2. **Conflating carbon neutrality with net-zero**: "Carbon neutral" (current offset-based
   balance) and "net-zero" (long-term structural elimination of emissions per IPCC/SBTi
   definition) are distinct. Using them interchangeably in public claims is misleading and
   may be actionable.

3. **Scope 3 selectively reported**: Reporting only favorable Scope 3 categories (e.g.,
   business travel) while omitting material upstream (Category 1) or downstream (Category 11) categories. ESRS E1 and IFRS S2 require disclosure of all material categories.

4. **Double materiality treated as financial materiality**: Conducting only an outside-in
   financial risk analysis for a CSRD report. CSRD requires impact materiality as an equal
   and independent lens. An ESRS materiality assessment that mirrors an ISSB/TCFD assessment
   is likely deficient.

5. **E1 claimed immaterial without rigorous analysis**: ESRS E1 carries a rebuttable
   presumption of materiality. Claiming climate is not material without detailed forward-
   looking qualitative analysis and scenario testing will not survive assurance scrutiny.

6. **Market-based Scope 2 only (without location-based)**: ESRS E1 and IFRS S2 require
   both market-based and location-based Scope 2 reporting. Reporting only market-based (to
   show low emissions due to RECs) without disclosing location-based fails both standards.

7. **ESRS 2 General Disclosures skipped on materiality grounds**: ESRS 2 (GOV/SBP/IRO
   disclosures) is mandatory without a materiality filter. Organizations that skip ESRS 2
   disclosures because "no topics are material" have misapplied the standard.

8. **Value chain scope limited to direct suppliers in ESRS**: ESRS E1 and S2 require
   disclosure of impacts, risks, and opportunities across the full value chain — upstream
   and downstream — not just Tier 1 suppliers. CSDDD limits substantive due diligence to
   Tier 1, but CSRD reporting has broader value chain scope.

9. **Transition plan without board approval or financial linkage**: A transition plan that
   is not approved by the board, not linked to CapEx/OpEx plans, and has no executive
   compensation linkage is a disclosure without substance — and assurance providers will
   challenge it.

10. **SBTi "committed" status treated as target achieved**: Being "SBTi committed" (i.e.,
    having signed a commitment letter) is not the same as having validated targets. Reporting
    "SBTi-aligned targets" based only on a commitment letter is inaccurate.

11. **Greenwashing in product advertising not reviewed alongside CSRD sustainability report**:
    Organizations that diligently prepare their CSRD sustainability statement but allow their
    marketing teams to publish unsupported green claims in advertising face significant
    enforcement risk under Directive 2024/825 — which applies to advertising, not just reports.

12. **Taxonomy eligibility disclosed without alignment**: Reporting a high percentage of
    Taxonomy-eligible revenue without separately disclosing that little of that eligible
    revenue is actually Taxonomy-aligned is misleading. Eligibility and alignment must both
    be disclosed, with the distinction explained.

13. **Assurance engagement started without data governance infrastructure**: Engaging an
    IASP for limited assurance without first implementing internal controls over sustainability
    data will almost certainly result in a qualified opinion or inability to complete
    assurance — often just before the annual report deadline.

14. **Comparing restatement years to show "progress"**: Restating prior-year emissions
    (correctly, in response to acquisitions or methodology changes per GHG Protocol) and then
    claiming the year-on-year absolute reduction as "progress" is misleading if the reduction
    is attributable to the restatement rather than actual decarbonization.

15. **CSDDD due diligence confused with CSRD reporting**: CSDDD creates substantive legal
    obligations to act on supply chain risks. CSRD requires reporting on those actions.
    Producing a polished CSRD supply chain disclosure without implementing the underlying
    CSDDD due diligence program satisfies reporting but not the substantive law.

16. **Treating CSRD Omnibus I as definitively reducing all obligations**: While Omnibus I
    narrows CSRD scope significantly, the final Amended ESRS standards (EC adoption due
    September 2026) are not yet in force. Organizations that prematurely reduce their data
    collection based on expected simplifications risk being non-compliant if the Amended
    ESRS retain more data points than anticipated.

17. **Using TCFD framework alone for CSRD compliance**: TCFD is incorporated into IFRS S2
    but does not satisfy CSRD/ESRS requirements. CSRD requires ESRS-format disclosures,
    double materiality, specific data points, and an iXBRL-tagged sustainability statement
    published in the management report. TCFD reports prepared for UK or investor purposes do
    not substitute for CSRD compliance.

18. **Net-zero target set for 2060 or later without interim milestones**: SBTi and major
    investor frameworks require net-zero by 2050 at the latest, with explicit near-term
    targets (5-10 year horizon) and interim milestones. A "net-zero 2060" target is not
    SBTi-compliant and will attract negative ratings from major ESG data providers.

19. **Biodiversity treated as entirely voluntary**: ESRS E4 is subject to materiality, but
    for organizations with significant land use, agriculture, or extractive operations,
    biodiversity is often material under both impact materiality (degradation of ecosystems)
    and financial materiality (regulatory and licence-to-operate risk). Dismissing ESRS E4
    as "voluntary" without rigorous materiality assessment is an error.

20. **SEC climate rules treated as fully rescinded and dismissed**: The SEC climate rules
    (Release Nos. 33-11275/34-99678) have been stayed but not formally rescinded via APA
    notice-and-comment rulemaking. Companies that have already complied or made disclosures
    under the rules should maintain those disclosures carefully; misrepresentation of climate
    risks can still trigger Rule 10b-5 claims regardless of the status of the mandatory
    disclosure regime.

## Writing Standards

Apply these standards before delivering any output:

1. **Cite specifically**: Every regulatory claim cites the specific directive, article, and
   paragraph — not a general reference to "CSRD" or "ESRS." Example: "ESRS E1, Disclosure
   Requirement E1-1, paragraph 16(a)" not "ESRS E1 requires transition plan disclosure."

2. **Distinguish mandatory from voluntary**: Always make explicit whether a disclosure
   is mandatory under an enacted law, mandatory under implementing regulations, subject to
   materiality assessment, voluntary under a reporting framework, or best practice only.

3. **Flag legislative flux explicitly**: The ESG regulatory landscape is in a period of
   significant flux (Omnibus I, Amended ESRS, FCA CP26/5, SEC stayed rules, SB 261
   injunction). Mark all references to pending or uncertain provisions with [VERIFY] and
   state the specific uncertainty. Do not present uncertain requirements as settled law.

4. **Use parallel structure for multi-jurisdiction analysis**: When analyzing the same
   topic across EU/UK/US, present findings in a consistent parallel format so users can
   scan horizontally for the same element across jurisdictions.

5. **Distinguish current requirement from current status**: A legal requirement may be
   enacted (current requirement) but not yet in force, stayed, or undergoing amendment
   (current status). Always state both.

6. **Quantify where possible**: When identifying a gap, provide the order of magnitude
   where helpful — e.g., "Scope 3 Category 1 typically represents 40-70% of total Scope
   3 for consumer goods companies; leaving it unquantified leaves the largest portion of
   the inventory undisclosed."

7. **Use active voice and specific verbs**: "The transition plan omits CapEx commitments
   beyond 2030" not "There may be areas where the transition plan could be improved in
   relation to long-term capital planning."

8. **No false precision on regulatory timelines**: Do not give a precise deadline (e.g.,
   "by 15 April 2028") without a source. If the deadline is derived from a transposition
   date or Omnibus I provision, cite the source and note where Member State implementing
   law may vary.

## Localization Notes

### EU — Core Jurisdiction

This skill is built around EU CSRD/ESRS as the primary mandatory framework. EU analysis
is the most comprehensive. Key EU-specific elements:

- **Member State transposition variations**: CSRD is a directive requiring Member State
  transposition; implementation details (penalties, enforcement mechanisms, exemptions for
  subsidiaries of group reporters) vary by Member State. [JURISDICTION-SPECIFIC: verify
  national implementing law for the specific Member State where the entity is incorporated.]
- **Listed SME treatment**: Omnibus I proposes but has not yet definitively resolved
  whether listed SMEs are permanently or temporarily exempted. [VERIFY current status
  post-Omnibus I before advising listed SMEs.]
- **ESRS sector-specific standards**: The original CSRD included mandatory sector-specific
  ESRS; the Omnibus I/Amended ESRS approach eliminates these. [VERIFY final Amended ESRS
  content once EC adopts by September 2026.]

### UK — Post-Brexit Framework

The UK has chosen not to adopt EU CSRD or EU Taxonomy. Key divergences:

- UK SRS S1/S2 (published February 2026) are voluntary, with mandatory application proposed
  from 1 January 2027 (FCA CP26/5). Final rules expected Autumn 2026. [VERIFY FCA final rules.]
- UK Green Taxonomy formally abandoned (15 July 2025). UK policy now focuses on transition
  plans and UK SRS adoption.
- SECR requirements (SI 2018/1155) remain in force for large UK companies — do not confuse
  with voluntary TCFD or UK SRS.
- The UK CMA may fine companies up to 10% of global turnover for misleading environmental
  claims (since April 2025).

### US — Fragmented and Litigation-Intensive

The US presents the most jurisdictionally complex and legally uncertain landscape:

- SEC climate rules are stayed; advise against relying on them for 2024/2025 reporting
  until APA rescission is resolved. [VERIFY current SEC status every reporting cycle.]
- California SB 253 is the most significant active mandatory requirement; CARB first filing
  deadline is 10 August 2026 for Scope 1/2. [VERIFY CARB regulatory updates regularly.]
- California SB 261 is currently enjoined; a climate financial risk report deadline has
  passed but CARB confirmed non-enforcement. [VERIFY Ninth Circuit decision when issued.]
- Anti-ESG legislation in 20+ US states creates compliance tension for companies reporting
  voluntarily. Legal review of the specific state portfolio is advisable for multinational
  companies with US operations.
- First Amendment challenges to mandatory climate disclosure are live (SB 261 Ninth Circuit).

### Global Voluntary Frameworks

- **ISSB adoption**: 21+ jurisdictions have adopted ISSB-based mandatory standards as of
  March 2026, including Australia (ASRS 101), Singapore, Hong Kong, Japan (from FY 2027),
  Brazil, Malaysia, and others. [JURISDICTION-SPECIFIC: verify specific jurisdiction's
  ISSB adoption timeline and any local modifications.]
- **GRI**: Voluntary; widely used globally; ESRS data points partially aligned with GRI
  standards; not sufficient alone for CSRD compliance.
