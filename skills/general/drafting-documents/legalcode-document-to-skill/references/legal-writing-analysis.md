# Learning professional legal writing

Read this when extracting or refining writing guidance. The output is this lawyer's usable writing profile. Describe supported choices concretely enough that another drafter can apply them to new facts while maintaining professional quality.

## Select and attribute the evidence

Prefer approved professional writing of the intended genre, audience and drafting side. Formal legal documents are valid evidence of the lawyer's deliberate voice. A client advice letter, pleading and contract may share vocabulary but perform different jobs; do not flatten their differences into one tone.

Record sample identifiers, relevant sections, document status and known authorship in the private source map. Distinguish the lawyer's own prose from quoted legislation, cited judgments, counterparty language, co-author contributions and editorial revisions. Where authorship is uncertain, describe a document or house pattern rather than attributing it to an individual.

One sample supports observations about that document, with provisional scope. Repetition across independent examples strengthens evidence, but identical precedent text in several files is not several independent authorial choices. A lawyer's express preference can settle a point that frequency cannot.

## Analyse the choices that make the writing recognisable

| Dimension | Look for | Turn it into guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Voice and perspective | Who speaks, how the client and recipient are named, professional distance, explanatory or argumentative stance | The supported narrative perspective and its context |
| Contextual tone | Restraint, courtesy, directness, firmness, urgency and formality in different sections or audiences | How a factual narrative, reservation, recommendation and request should differ |
| Vocabulary | Preferred terms and word combinations, attribution and reasoning verbs, connective phrases, defined terms, opening and closing formulas | Positive phrase patterns with their function and limits |
| Sentence construction | Clause order, qualification placement, active/passive choices, parentheticals, rhythm and sentence variation | Representative constructions and when they are useful |
| Paragraph development | Topic sentences, proposition/evidence/application sequence, transitions, counterarguments and conclusions | A supported order of thought, without requiring every paragraph to follow it |
| Professional conventions | Citation placement, quotations, abbreviations, emphasis, dates, numbers and attachments | The actual conventions, with examples where ambiguous |

Pay particular attention to sentences that attribute an allegation, state an established fact, introduce a rule, apply it, acknowledge a limitation, reserve a right or recommend an action. These functions reveal more than adjectives such as “formal” or “authoritative”. Do not add functions absent from the evidence just to complete the table.

Separate fixed approved formulas from flexible construction patterns. Keep operative standard wording in the template. A phrase used to state an obligation cannot freely replace a phrase expressing intention or discretion. Preserve defined terms even where stylistic variation would otherwise be natural.

Sentence lengths and phrase frequency may describe the corpus; they are not quotas. Nonappearance is not a prohibition. Avoid personality archetypes, numerical voice scores and blanket instructions to sound more confident. Certainty follows the evidence and the legal function of the sentence.

## Write the generated legal-writing profile

Use `references/legal-writing-profile.md` when the document contains substantial new prose. Keep a short form's small set of rules in its SKILL.md if that is clearer. The profile should contain only supported observations that affect drafting:

1. Scope: document, audience, drafting side, language and evidence limitations.
2. Voice and contextual tone: perspective and the circumstances that change the register.
3. Vocabulary and sentence functions: preferred terms, phrase patterns and qualifications.
4. Construction: sentence, paragraph and argument development with examples.
5. Professional conventions and any unresolved conflicts.

For each meaningful pattern, record an actionable instruction, where it applies, evidence, status and limits. Status can be observed, inferred, user-stated or confirmed; include occurrence counts only when informative. Do not call an observed pattern mandatory unless the lawyer or a verified external requirement makes it so. Keep detailed identifying evidence private; a shared profile can use safe source identifiers and sanitised excerpts.

Use short annotated examples that preserve the original construction while replacing names, figures, matter facts and identifiable combinations. An illustration teaches expression; it never supplies a fact, authority or conclusion for a new matter. Where legal wording cannot safely be altered, select a different example or obtain an appropriate approved excerpt.

For calibration, compare plausible versions of the same content. Hold facts, attribution, legal force and certainty constant so the comparison tests style. For example, these invented sentences illustrate placement of attribution, not a recommended house style:

- “The client states that it delivered the records on 12 May.”
- “According to the client, the records were delivered on 12 May.”

Both attribute the account to the client. “The records were delivered on 12 May” removes that attribution and cannot be treated as a merely stylistic alternative. Choose patterns from the real samples; do not copy this example into every profile.

## Apply professional quality independently

The draft should sound like the lawyer and meet a high professional standard. Assess precision of terms, clear actors and obligations, grammatical correctness, logical connections, proportionate emphasis, appropriate courtesy and consistent treatment of uncertainty. Professional writing may be formal, concise or detailed as its purpose requires.

Preserve deliberate, accurate formal usage. Do not reproduce typographical mistakes, accidental repetition, inconsistent terminology or empty emphasis as habits. Correct clear expression defects in newly drafted prose where meaning is unchanged; flag ambiguous wording whose intended meaning cannot be established. Identify proposed corrections to approved fixed wording separately rather than silently changing the precedent. Do not silently resolve a suspected substantive error, outdated formula, deficient authority or disputed position under the label of style.

Before accepting a stylistic rewrite, compare the propositions: speaker, fact or allegation, source, certainty, actor, obligation or permission, conditions, exceptions, deadlines, remedies and conclusion. A change to any of those is substantive and needs its own justification. The profile cannot manufacture missing evidence or reasoning.

If the requested preference conflicts with a sample, record the conflict and resolution. Respect an explicit preference within its stated scope. If its application would change legal meaning, surface that consequence before encoding it as reusable guidance.
