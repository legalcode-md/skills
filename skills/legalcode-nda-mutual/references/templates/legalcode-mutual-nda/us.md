# Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement — United States

This Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement ("**Agreement**") is made and entered
into as of {{Effective Date}} (the "**Effective Date**") by and between
**{{Party A Legal Name}}**, a {{Party A Entity Type}} organized under the
laws of {{Party A Country}}, with its principal place of business at
{{Party A Address}} ("**{{Party A Short Name}}**"), and
**{{Party B Legal Name}}**, a {{Party B Entity Type}} organized under the
laws of {{Party B Country}}, with its principal place of business at
{{Party B Address}} ("**{{Party B Short Name}}**"). {{Party A Short Name}}
and {{Party B Short Name}} are each a "**Party**" and together the
"**Parties**."

---

## 1. Plain-language summary

In plain English: the Parties want to discuss something specific, and
during those discussions one or both may share non-public information.
Each Party agrees to keep the other's confidential information
confidential, use it only for the stated purpose, and share it only
with people who need to know. Trade secrets stay protected for as long
as they remain trade secrets under federal and state law. Standard
exclusions apply (public information, prior knowledge, independent
development, and rightful third-party disclosures). Nothing here
limits any individual's rights under federal or state whistleblower
laws, including 18 U.S.C. § 1833(b).

This summary is for orientation only. Sections 2–16 control if there
is any inconsistency.

## 2. Parties & purpose

The Parties enter into this Agreement to evaluate a specific business
opportunity. The Parties wish to explore {{Purpose}} (the
"**Purpose**"), and in connection with the Purpose each Party (as
"**Discloser**") may disclose to the other (as "**Recipient**")
confidential or proprietary information. Either Party may act as
Discloser, Recipient, or both.

The Purpose is the only authorized use of Confidential Information and
shall be construed narrowly; any expansion or successor engagement
requires written agreement from both Parties. Annex A contains
particulars regarding the Purpose.

## 3. Definitions

This Section defines the key terms used in this Agreement. Capitalized
terms not defined elsewhere have the meanings given below.

**"Affiliate"** means an entity that controls, is controlled by, or is
under common control with a Party, where "control" means ownership of
more than fifty percent (50%) of the voting equity or the power to
direct management.

**"Confidential Information"** has the meaning set forth in Section 4.

**"Representatives"** means a Party's Affiliates, directors, officers,
employees, contractors, and professional advisors (including
attorneys, accountants, and financial advisors) who have a need to
know Confidential Information for the Purpose and who are bound by
confidentiality obligations at least as protective as those in this
Agreement.

**"Trade Secret"** means information that qualifies as a trade secret
under 18 U.S.C. § 1839(3) (the Defend Trade Secrets Act, "**DTSA**"):
all forms and types of financial, business, scientific, technical,
economic, or engineering information — including patterns, plans,
compilations, program devices, formulas, designs, prototypes, methods,
techniques, processes, procedures, programs, or codes, however stored
or memorialized — where (A) the owner has taken reasonable measures
to keep the information secret, and (B) the information derives
independent economic value, actual or potential, from not being
generally known to, and not being readily ascertainable through
proper means by, another person who can obtain economic value from
its disclosure or use. The term also includes information qualifying
as a trade secret under any applicable Uniform Trade Secrets Act
("**UTSA**") or common-law trade-secret doctrine, which generally
requires (i) information that derives economic value from not being
generally known and (ii) reasonable efforts to maintain secrecy.

**"DTSA"** means the Defend Trade Secrets Act of 2016, codified at
18 U.S.C. §§ 1831–1839, including the federal civil cause of action at
§ 1836(b) and the Economic Espionage Act criminal provisions at
§§ 1831–1832.

**"UTSA"** means the Uniform Trade Secrets Act as enacted (with state
variations) in {{State Of Choice Of Law}} and any other state whose
law applies to a dispute.

## 4. Confidential Information

This Section describes what information is protected. "Confidential
Information" is broadly defined and includes Trade Secrets, but
standard exclusions apply (see Section 7).

4.1 **Scope.** "**Confidential Information**" means any non-public
information disclosed by or on behalf of the Discloser to the
Recipient in connection with the Purpose, in any form or medium, that
is either (a) marked or identified as confidential or proprietary at
or near disclosure, or (b) information that a reasonable person,
given its nature and the circumstances of disclosure, would understand
to be confidential or proprietary.

4.2 **Examples.** Confidential Information may include, without
limitation: business plans and strategies; financial information,
forecasts, and pricing; product roadmaps, specifications, designs,
prototypes, and source code; algorithms, models, and know-how;
customer, supplier, and personnel data; security architecture and
incident information; the existence, contents, and status of the
Parties' discussions; and the terms of this Agreement.

4.3 **Trade Secrets.** Confidential Information may include Trade
Secrets within the meaning of 18 U.S.C. § 1839(3) and any applicable
state Uniform Trade Secrets Act. Trade Secrets receive the
protections in this Agreement and remain protected for the duration
described in Section 9, in addition to all statutory and common-law
protections that apply independently.

4.4 **Form of disclosure.** No specific marking is required for
information to qualify as Confidential Information, provided
Section 4.1 is satisfied. Where practicable, the Discloser will mark
tangible Confidential Information as "Confidential" and identify oral
disclosures as confidential at or near disclosure.

## 5. Permitted disclosures & use

This Section sets out what the Recipient may and may not do with
Confidential Information.

5.1 **Use limitation.** The Recipient shall use Confidential
Information solely for the Purpose. The Recipient shall not use
Confidential Information to compete with the Discloser, to reverse
engineer the Discloser's products or services, or to develop, market,
or sell products or services that incorporate or derive from the
Discloser's Confidential Information.

5.2 **Disclosure limitation.** The Recipient shall not disclose
Confidential Information to any person other than its Representatives
who have a need to know for the Purpose and who are bound by
confidentiality obligations at least as protective as this Agreement.

5.3 **Responsibility for Representatives.** The Recipient is
responsible for any breach of this Agreement by its Representatives
as if the Recipient had committed the breach itself.

5.4 **No reverse engineering.** The Recipient shall not, directly or
indirectly, decompile, disassemble, or reverse engineer any tangible
embodiment of the Confidential Information, except as expressly
permitted by non-waivable applicable law.

## 6. Standard of care

This Section requires the Recipient to protect Confidential Information
using the same care it uses for its own confidential information, and
in any event no less than a reasonable standard.

The Recipient shall protect Confidential Information using at least
the same degree of care it uses for its own confidential information
of similar nature and importance, and in no event less than a
reasonable degree of care. The Recipient shall (a) maintain
administrative, technical, and physical safeguards appropriate to the
sensitivity of the Confidential Information, including access
controls, encryption where appropriate, and audit logging; (b)
promptly notify the Discloser upon learning of any actual or suspected
unauthorized access, disclosure, or use; and (c) reasonably cooperate
with the Discloser to mitigate any such incident.

The Recipient's measures to protect Trade Secrets are intended to
support, among other things, the Discloser's obligation to take
"reasonable measures to keep such information secret" within
18 U.S.C. § 1839(3)(A).

## 7. Exclusions

This Section identifies categories of information that are not
Confidential Information, even if disclosed by the Discloser. The
Recipient bears the burden of proving that an exclusion applies.

Confidential Information does not include information that the
Recipient can demonstrate by competent written evidence:

(a) **Public domain** — was generally available to the public at the
time of disclosure, or thereafter became generally available through no
breach of this Agreement by the Recipient or its Representatives;

(b) **Prior knowledge** — was already known to the Recipient on a
non-confidential basis, without obligation of confidence to the
Discloser, prior to its receipt from the Discloser;

(c) **Third-party disclosure** — was rightfully received by the
Recipient from a third party who was not, to the Recipient's knowledge,
under any obligation of confidentiality with respect to the
information;

(d) **Independent development** — was independently developed by the
Recipient or its Representatives without reference to or use of the
Discloser's Confidential Information; and

(e) **FTC Non-Compete Rule (16 CFR Part 910)** `[VERIFY enforceability/commencement status]` —
to the extent any provision of this Agreement would, in operation,
function as a "non-compete clause" within the meaning of the FTC's
Non-Compete Rule (16 CFR Part 910), nothing in this Agreement is
intended to do so. The Parties acknowledge that the FTC's Non-Compete
Rule does not, by its own terms, prohibit non-disclosure agreements
that are reasonably tailored to protect Trade Secrets and confidential
business information; this Agreement is intended to fall within that
permitted scope. If any provision is held to be a prohibited
non-compete clause, that provision shall be reformed to the minimum
extent necessary to render it enforceable, with the remainder of this
Agreement unaffected.

## 8. Compelled disclosure

This Section addresses what happens when the Recipient is legally
required to disclose Confidential Information, for example by a
subpoena or court order.

8.1 **Permitted compelled disclosure.** If the Recipient is required
by applicable law, legal process, or governmental authority to
disclose Confidential Information — including a federal or state
subpoena; court order; civil investigative demand; regulatory inquiry
or request from the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission
("**SEC**"), Department of Justice ("**DOJ**"), Federal Trade
Commission ("**FTC**"), a state Attorney General, or a self-regulatory
organization; or disclosure obligations under the Securities Exchange
Act of 1934 (including Regulation FD) — the Recipient may disclose
only that portion legally required.

8.2 **Notice.** To the extent legally permitted and reasonably
practicable, the Recipient shall (a) give the Discloser prompt written
notice sufficient to allow the Discloser to seek a protective order
or other remedy, and (b) reasonably cooperate, at the Discloser's
expense, with the Discloser's efforts to obtain that relief or limit
disclosure.

8.3 **Public-records and FOIA.** If the Discloser is or contracts
with a government entity subject to the Freedom of Information Act,
5 U.S.C. § 552, or an analogous state public-records or sunshine law,
the Recipient shall reasonably cooperate to identify Confidential
Information that may qualify for an applicable exemption (including
trade-secret and commercial-information exemptions) and to support
required markings, redactions, or submissions.

8.4 **Continuing protection.** Compelled disclosure does not change
the confidential status of the disclosed information as between the
Parties, and the Recipient remains bound by this Agreement with
respect to that information for all other purposes.

## 9. Term & duration of obligations

This Section explains how long the Recipient's confidentiality
obligations last. Trade Secret protection continues for as long as the
information remains a Trade Secret.

9.1 **Disclosure Period.** The "**Disclosure Period**" begins on the
Effective Date and ends on the earlier of (a) {{Disclosure Period}}
after the Effective Date (default: two (2) years) and (b) termination
of this Agreement. Information disclosed after the Disclosure Period
is not Confidential Information under this Agreement unless the
Parties agree in writing.

9.2 **Confidentiality Term.** The Recipient's obligations under
Sections 5, 6, 8, and 11 with respect to non-Trade-Secret Confidential
Information continue for {{Confidentiality Term}} from the date of
disclosure (default: five (5) years).

9.3 **Trade-Secret carve-out.** With respect to Confidential
Information that constitutes a Trade Secret, the Recipient's
obligations continue for so long as the information remains a Trade
Secret under the DTSA (18 U.S.C. § 1839(3)) or any applicable state
Uniform Trade Secrets Act.

9.4 **Termination.** Either Party may terminate this Agreement for
convenience on thirty (30) days' written notice. Termination does not
affect obligations with respect to Confidential Information already
disclosed (which continue per Sections 9.2 and 9.3) or any accrued
rights or remedies.

## 10. Return or destruction of materials

This Section explains what happens to Confidential Information at the
end of the engagement.

10.1 **On request.** Upon written request from the Discloser, and in
any event upon termination or expiration, the Recipient shall, within
{{Return Window}} (default: thirty (30) days), at the Discloser's
election, (a) return all tangible Confidential Information or (b)
destroy it and provide written certification of destruction signed by
an authorized officer.

10.2 **Backup and legal-hold exceptions.** The Recipient may retain
Confidential Information (i) on routine backup or archival systems not
readily accessible in the ordinary course, (ii) as required to comply
with applicable law, regulation, or bona fide legal-hold obligations,
and (iii) one archival copy for administering and enforcing this
Agreement. Retained Confidential Information remains subject to this
Agreement until destroyed or returned.

10.3 **Representatives' copies.** The Recipient is responsible for
its Representatives' compliance with this Section.

## 11. No license; ownership

This Section makes clear that disclosing Confidential Information does
not grant the Recipient any rights in it.

11.1 **No license.** Each Party retains all right, title, and interest
in its own Confidential Information. No license, express or implied,
is granted to the Recipient under any patent, copyright, trademark,
trade-secret, or other intellectual-property right, except the
limited right to use the Confidential Information for the Purpose
during the Disclosure Period.

11.2 **No obligation to proceed.** Neither Party is obligated to enter
into any further agreement or business relationship. Either may
terminate discussions at any time.

11.3 **No representations or warranties.** Confidential Information is
provided "as is." The Discloser makes no representation or warranty
regarding accuracy, completeness, fitness for a particular purpose,
or non-infringement, and assumes no liability arising from the
Recipient's use of or reliance on Confidential Information, except as
expressly set forth in this Agreement or a subsequent signed writing.

## 12. Residuals (OPTIONAL)

This Section addresses information retained in the unaided memory of
personnel. It applies only if expressly elected by the Parties.

OPTIONAL — only if the Parties expressly elect to include a residuals
clause; otherwise, omit this Section.

Subject to Sections 5, 7, and 9.3, the Recipient and its
Representatives shall not be precluded from using "Residuals" —
information in non-tangible form retained in the unaided memory of an
individual who had access to Confidential Information in compliance
with this Agreement, where "unaided memory" means without intentional
memorization and without reference to any tangible embodiment. This
Section grants no license under any patent, copyright, or registered
trademark, and does not authorize the use of any Trade Secret.

## 13. Remedies & equitable relief

This Section addresses what each Party can do if the other breaches.
Money damages may not be enough; injunctive relief may be available;
and the DTSA provides additional remedies in trade-secret cases.

13.1 **Equitable relief.** The Parties acknowledge that a breach of
this Agreement may cause irreparable harm for which monetary damages
are inadequate. The non-breaching Party shall be entitled to seek
specific performance and temporary, preliminary, and permanent
injunctive relief, without the need to post a bond or prove actual
damages, except where required by non-waivable law.

13.2 **DTSA remedies.** In any civil action under the DTSA
(18 U.S.C. § 1836(b)), the prevailing Party may seek the statutory
remedies, including (a) injunctive relief under § 1836(b)(3)(A);
(b) damages for actual loss and unjust enrichment, or in lieu a
reasonable royalty, under § 1836(b)(3)(B); (c) exemplary damages of
up to two times actual damages for willful and malicious
misappropriation under § 1836(b)(3)(C); and (d) reasonable attorneys'
fees and costs for willful and malicious misappropriation, bad-faith
claims, or bad-faith motions to terminate an injunction under
§ 1836(b)(3)(D). The Whistleblower Notice in Section 16.6 is provided
in compliance with 18 U.S.C. § 1833(b)(3) so that exemplary damages
and attorneys' fees remain available.

13.3 **State UTSA remedies.** Each Party retains the right to seek
remedies under any applicable state Uniform Trade Secrets Act,
including those of {{State Of Choice Of Law}}.

13.4 **California carve-out — Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 16600.** With
respect to any individual residing in or primarily employed in
California, this Agreement shall be construed and enforced consistent
with California Business and Professions Code § 16600 and authority
including _Edwards v. Arthur Andersen LLP_, 44 Cal. 4th 937 (2008).
This Agreement is not intended to operate as a covenant not to
compete or to restrain any individual from a lawful profession,
trade, or business. Injunctive relief involving such an individual
shall be narrowly tailored to prevent misuse or disclosure of
Confidential Information and Trade Secrets, and not to prevent the
individual from working for any particular employer or field.

13.5 **Cumulative remedies.** Remedies under this Agreement are
cumulative and in addition to all other remedies at law or in equity.

This Section identifies the law that governs this Agreement and where

15.1 **Default — Delaware.** Except as provided in Sections 15.3–15.5,
this Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of
{{State Of Choice Of Law}} (default: Delaware), without regard to

15.2 **Forum.** Each Party consents to exclusive jurisdiction of the
{{Court Of Forum}} (default: Delaware Court of Chancery) for actions

an individual residing in or primarily employed in California,

non-waivable basis. Any prevailing-party fee provision in this

15.4 **New York carve-out — N.Y. Gen. Oblig. L. § 5-1401.** If the
Parties select New York law, they rely upon N.Y. Gen. Oblig. L.

15.6 **Jury-trial waiver.** To the extent permitted by applicable
law, each Party irrevocably waives any right to a trial by jury.

## 16. Miscellaneous

This Section contains standard contractual housekeeping plus the
mandatory whistleblower-immunity notice required to preserve DTSA
remedies, and several US-specific clauses.

16.1 **Entire agreement.** This Agreement, with its Annexes, is the
entire agreement between the Parties on its subject matter and
supersedes all prior or contemporaneous agreements and understandings,
written or oral. It does not supersede any separate existing
confidentiality agreement to the extent that agreement is more
protective of the Discloser's Confidential Information.

16.2 **Amendments and waiver.** No amendment or waiver is effective
unless in writing and signed by both Parties. A failure or delay in
exercising any right does not operate as a waiver.

16.3 **Assignment.** Neither Party may assign this Agreement without
the other's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld);
provided that either Party may assign without consent in connection
with a merger, acquisition, reorganization, or sale of all or
substantially all of its assets, so long as the assignee assumes the
obligations in writing. Any purported assignment in violation of this
Section is void.

16.4 **Severability.** If any provision is held unenforceable, the
remainder of the Agreement continues in effect and the unenforceable
provision shall be reformed to the minimum extent necessary to render
it enforceable while preserving the Parties' intent.

16.5 **Counterparts; electronic signatures.** This Agreement may be
executed in counterparts, each deemed an original and together one
instrument. Electronic signatures (including DocuSign, Adobe Sign, or
PDF) are deemed original signatures, consistent with the Electronic
Signatures in Global and National Commerce Act (15 U.S.C. §§ 7001
et seq.) and the Uniform Electronic Transactions Act.

16.6 **18 U.S.C. § 1833(b) Notice — Whistleblower Immunity.**
Notwithstanding any other provision of this Agreement, the Parties
provide the following notice as required by 18 U.S.C. § 1833(b)(3):

> **18 U.S.C. § 1833(b) Notice — Whistleblower Immunity:** An
> individual shall not be held criminally or civilly liable under any
> Federal or State trade-secret law for the disclosure of a trade
> secret that — (A) is made (i) in confidence to a Federal, State, or
> local government official, either directly or indirectly, or to an
> attorney; and (ii) solely for the purpose of reporting or
> investigating a suspected violation of law; or (B) is made in a
> complaint or other document filed in a lawsuit or other proceeding,
> if such filing is made under seal. An individual who files a lawsuit
> for retaliation by an employer for reporting a suspected violation
> of law may disclose the trade secret to the attorney of the
> individual and use the trade-secret information in the court
> proceeding, if the individual — (A) files any document containing
> the trade secret under seal; and (B) does not disclose the trade
> secret, except pursuant to court order.

The block above is US public-domain statutory text from 18 U.S.C.
§ 1833(b). This notice is provided to preserve exemplary damages and
attorneys' fees under 18 U.S.C. § 1836(b)(3) in any DTSA action
against an individual acting for either Party, and to satisfy
18 U.S.C. § 1833(b)(3)(A)–(B) for any individual party in their
personal capacity. It supplements (does not replace) the equivalent
notice that should appear in employment, contractor, and consulting
agreements `[VERIFY scope]` — each Party should confirm its own
employment and contractor agreements include the same notice.

16.7 **No retaliation.** Nothing in this Agreement limits any
individual from (a) reporting possible violations of law to a
governmental agency or self-regulatory organization, or making
disclosures protected under federal or state whistleblower laws
(including SEC, OSHA, NLRB, and EEOC reporting); (b) participating in
any governmental investigation; or (c) discussing wages, hours, or
other terms of employment protected by Section 7 of the NLRA. No
prior authorization or notice is required.

16.8 **Securities trading restrictions** (OPTIONAL — only if material
non-public information ("**MNPI**") about a publicly-traded issuer
may be exchanged). The Recipient acknowledges Confidential Information
may constitute MNPI under Section 10(b) of the Exchange Act and Rule
10b-5. The Recipient shall not, and shall cause its Representatives
not to, trade in any security of the Discloser or any other issuer to
which the MNPI relates while in possession of MNPI, and shall comply
with applicable insider-trading laws and policies.

16.9 **Export controls.** Confidential Information may be subject to
U.S. export-control laws, including the Export Administration
Regulations (15 C.F.R. Parts 730–774) ("**EAR**"), the International
Traffic in Arms Regulations (22 C.F.R. Parts 120–130) ("**ITAR**"),
and OFAC sanctions regulations. The Recipient shall not export,
re-export, transfer, or disclose Confidential Information in violation
of those laws and shall obtain any required license or authorization.

16.10 **Government rights carve-out** (OPTIONAL — only if either
Party is a U.S. government contractor and the discussions may relate
to a government contract). Nothing in this Agreement diminishes U.S.
Government rights in technical data or computer software delivered

equivalent FAR or DFARS clause (including DFARS 252.227-7013 and
252.227-7014). Government rights remain governed by the applicable
FAR/DFARS clauses; this Agreement applies only to the extent not
inconsistent with them.

16.11 **Headings; construction.** Headings are for convenience only.
"Include," "includes," and "including" mean "without limitation."

16.12 **Independent contractors.** The Parties are independent
contractors. Nothing creates a partnership, joint venture, employment,
agency, or fiduciary relationship.
