# Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement — EU/EEA Variant

This template is calibrated for the EU/EEA, aligned to Directive (EU) 2016/943 (the "Trade Secrets Directive"), GDPR, eIDAS Regulation (EU) No 910/2014, and the Brussels I bis / Rome I framework. A Germany-specific appendix at §17 covers BGB AGB-Kontrolle and the Geschäftsgeheimnisgesetz (GeschGehG). Merge fields use Mustache syntax. `[VERIFY]` flags mark items requiring Member-State-specific review.

---

## 1. Plain-language summary

In short: each Party may share sensitive business information with the other for a defined purpose, and each promises to keep the other side's information confidential, use it only for that purpose, and take reasonable steps to protect it. Some material may qualify as a trade secret under EU law and receives heightened protection. Public-interest disclosures, whistleblowing, and worker-representative disclosures are preserved by law. Personal data is governed by GDPR and, where processing is involved, a separate data processing agreement.

This summary is for orientation only and does not modify the operative clauses below.

## 3. Definitions

Plain-English summary: defines the key terms used below, including what counts as a trade secret under EU law.

3.1 **"Affiliate"** means any entity that directly or indirectly controls, is controlled by, or is under common control with a Party, where "control" means ownership of more than 50% of the voting rights or the power to direct management.

3.2 **"Confidential Information"** has the meaning given in §4.

3.3 **"Disclosing Party"** / **"Receiving Party"** mean, respectively, the Party (or its Affiliate or Representative) disclosing or receiving Confidential Information.

3.4 **"Representative"** means a Party's directors, officers, employees, contractors, professional advisers, auditors, and Affiliates who have a need to know for the Purpose and are bound by confidentiality obligations no less protective than those here.

3.5 **"Trade Secret"** means information meeting all three conditions in Article 2(1) of Directive (EU) 2016/943: (a) it is secret — not generally known among or readily accessible to persons within the circles that normally deal with the kind of information in question; (b) it has commercial value because it is secret; and (c) it has been subject to reasonable steps under the circumstances, by the person lawfully in control, to keep it secret.

3.6 **"Personal Data"** has the meaning given in Article 4(1) GDPR.

3.7 **"Controlling Member State"** means `{{Controlling Member State}}`, whose national law transposing the Trade Secrets Directive applies as set out in §15.

## 4. Confidential Information

Plain-English summary: confidential information is anything the other side shares that is marked confidential or is obviously confidential, including trade secrets.

4.1 "Confidential Information" means any information disclosed by or on behalf of the Disclosing Party in connection with the Purpose, in any form (oral, written, electronic, visual, or otherwise), that is either (a) identified as confidential at or around the time of disclosure, or (b) of a nature or disclosed in circumstances such that a reasonable recipient would understand it to be confidential.

4.2 It includes, without limitation, technical, commercial, financial, operational, customer, supplier, product, research, security, source-code, algorithm, dataset, pricing, strategic, and personnel information, and the existence and contents of the discussions between the Parties.

4.3 Confidential Information **may include Trade Secrets** within the meaning of Article 2(1) of Directive (EU) 2016/943. Where any Confidential Information is, or is treated by the Disclosing Party as, a Trade Secret, the Receiving Party shall take reasonable steps under the circumstances to maintain secrecy as required by Article 2(1)(c) of that Directive, including the controls described in §6.

4.4 Absence of a confidentiality marking does not, by itself, defeat the confidential character of information that is otherwise apparently confidential.

## 5. Permitted disclosures & use

Plain-English summary: each side may use the other's information only for the agreed purpose, and share it only with people who need to know.

5.1 The Receiving Party shall use Confidential Information solely for the Purpose and shall not use it for any other purpose, including any commercial advantage outside the Purpose.

5.2 The Receiving Party may disclose Confidential Information only to its Representatives who (a) have a genuine need to know for the Purpose and (b) are bound by written or professional confidentiality obligations at least as protective as those in this Agreement.

5.3 Disclosures to Representatives shall constitute "reasonable steps under the circumstances" within Article 2(1)(c) of the Trade Secrets Directive, including need-to-know access, recordkeeping, and return-or-destruction obligations on outgoing personnel.

5.4 The Receiving Party is responsible for any breach by its Representatives as if it were its own.

5.5 OPTIONAL — only if Affiliate sharing is selected: The Receiving Party may disclose to its Affiliates on the same terms, remaining liable for their compliance.

## 6. Standard of care

Plain-English summary: each side must protect the other's confidential information at least as carefully as its own, and in any event with reasonable care.

6.1 The Receiving Party shall protect Confidential Information with a degree of care no less than (a) the care it applies to its own confidential information of like sensitivity, or (b) reasonable care under the circumstances, whichever is higher.

6.2 Reasonable steps include, as appropriate: access controls and authentication, encryption in transit and at rest, written undertakings from Representatives, secure storage, controlled copying, and prompt remediation of any suspected unauthorised access or disclosure.

6.3 The Receiving Party shall keep records sufficient to demonstrate compliance with Article 2(1)(c) of the Trade Secrets Directive, including a list of Representatives with access and a description of the technical and organisational measures applied.

6.4 The Receiving Party shall notify the Disclosing Party without undue delay, and in any event within `{{Breach Notification Hours}}` hours, of any actual or reasonably suspected unauthorised acquisition, use, or disclosure, and shall cooperate in good faith in any investigation or mitigation.

## 7. Exclusions

Plain-English summary: information is not protected if already public, already known, independently developed, or covered by a legally recognised public-interest carve-out.

7.1 The obligations do not apply to information that the Receiving Party can demonstrate by contemporaneous written records: (a) is or becomes generally available to the public other than through a breach of this Agreement; (b) was known to the Receiving Party, free of any duty of confidence, before disclosure; (c) is rightfully received from a third party not, to the Receiving Party's knowledge, bound by any duty of confidence; or (d) is independently developed by the Receiving Party without use of or reference to the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information.

7.2 In addition, consistent with Article 5 of Directive (EU) 2016/943, the obligations in this Agreement do not restrict, prohibit, or penalise:

(e) **Trade Secrets Directive Art. 5 carve-outs** — disclosure or acquisition of information for the purpose of (i) exercising the right to freedom of expression and information, including respect for media freedom and pluralism under the Charter; (ii) revealing misconduct, wrongdoing, or illegal activity in the public interest (whistleblowing); (iii) disclosure by workers to their representatives in the legitimate exercise of representational functions under Union or national law `[VERIFY transposition in Controlling Member State]`; or (iv) protecting a legitimate interest recognised by Union or national law.

(f) **GDPR cross-reference** — disclosure required to give effect to a data subject's right of access under Article 15 GDPR or any other right under Chapter III GDPR. Processing of any Personal Data exchanged between the Parties remains subject to GDPR and to a separate data processing agreement (see the companion `legalcode-dpa` template).

7.3 Nothing here waives any right or protection conferred on whistleblowers, workers, workers' representatives, or journalists by Union or national law.

## 8. Compelled disclosure

Plain-English summary: if a court, regulator, or other competent authority orders disclosure, the receiving side may comply but should give notice and seek protective treatment where lawful.

8.1 The Receiving Party may disclose Confidential Information to the extent strictly required by: (a) a national court order; (b) a binding regulatory request (sectoral regulator, competition, securities, or tax authority); (c) a supervisory-authority order under Article 58 GDPR; (d) an incident-reporting obligation under Directive (EU) 2022/2555 ("NIS2") or its national transposition; (e) for regulated entities, a request from the ECB, a national central bank, or a national competent authority under Regulation (EU) No 1024/2013 or sectoral financial-services legislation; or (f) any other binding legal process.

8.2 Where lawful and reasonably practicable, the Receiving Party shall (i) give the Disclosing Party prompt advance written notice, (ii) cooperate with reasonable efforts to obtain a protective order or confidential-treatment ruling, and (iii) disclose only the portion of Confidential Information legally required to be disclosed.

8.3 Compelled disclosure does not, by itself, defeat the confidential character or Trade Secret status of the information.

## 9. Term & duration of obligations

Plain-English summary: information may be exchanged for a defined window; the confidentiality duty runs for a defined period after that, with a longer "as long as it remains a trade secret" tail for trade-secret material.

9.1 **Disclosure Period.** Confidential Information may be disclosed for `{{Disclosure-Period}}` (default: two (2) years) from the Effective Date, unless extended in writing or terminated earlier by either Party on `{{Termination Notice Days}}` days' written notice.

9.2 **Confidentiality Term.** §§4–6 apply during the Disclosure Period and continue for `{{Confidentiality-Term}}` (default: five (5) years) thereafter.

9.3 **Trade-Secret carve-out.** Notwithstanding §9.2, Confidential Information qualifying as a Trade Secret remains subject to this Agreement for so long as it continues to qualify under Article 2(1) of Directive (EU) 2016/943, with the qualification that, under §§ 305c and 307 BGB and equivalent national laws, indefinite confidentiality obligations may not bind a B2C counter-party for an unreasonable period; in such a case the obligation runs for the longest period reasonable under the applicable national law.

9.4 Termination does not affect rights, remedies, or obligations accrued before termination, nor any surviving provisions.

## 10. Return or destruction of materials

Plain-English summary: when the relationship ends or the disclosing side asks, the receiving side returns or deletes the materials and confirms in writing.

10.1 On the earlier of (a) expiry or termination of the Disclosure Period or (b) the Disclosing Party's written request, the Receiving Party shall, at the Disclosing Party's election, return or securely destroy all Confidential Information in its possession or control, including copies, extracts, and derivative materials.

10.2 The Receiving Party shall, on request, provide written certification by an authorised officer of compliance with §10.1.

10.3 The Receiving Party may retain Confidential Information (i) as required by law, regulation, audit, or recordkeeping obligations; (ii) in routine back-ups, archival, or disaster-recovery copies not readily accessible in the ordinary course; and (iii) in board-meeting or professional-adviser files. Retained material remains subject to this Agreement while retained.

## 11. No license; ownership

Plain-English summary: sharing information does not transfer any IP rights; each side keeps what it owns.

11.1 All Confidential Information remains the property of the Disclosing Party. Nothing in this Agreement transfers or grants, by implication, estoppel, or otherwise, any right, title, licence, or interest in any patents, trade marks, copyrights, database rights, design rights, trade secrets, or other IP rights, save for the limited right to use Confidential Information for the Purpose under §5.

11.2 Feedback voluntarily provided by the Receiving Party regarding the Disclosing Party's products or services may be used by the Disclosing Party without restriction, provided no Confidential Information of the Receiving Party is thereby disclosed.

## 12. Residuals (OPTIONAL)

Plain-English summary: if selected, lets individuals retain unaided memory of what they learned, but does not authorise misuse of trade secrets.

12.1 OPTIONAL — only if both Parties initial this §12: Subject to §11 and to Trade Secrets Directive protections, the Receiving Party is not restricted from using "Residuals" — information in non-tangible form incidentally retained in the unaided memory of an individual Representative with rightful access — provided (a) the use does not involve reproduction of, or reference to, the Disclosing Party's tangible Confidential Information, and (b) it does not involve misappropriation of any Trade Secret within Article 4 of Directive (EU) 2016/943.

12.2 §12 grants no licence under any patent, copyright, database right, or registered design.

## 13. Remedies & equitable relief

Plain-English summary: if confidential information is misused, the affected side may seek court orders to stop the misuse plus damages; the EU Trade Secrets Directive provides a specific menu of remedies.

13.1 A breach of this Agreement may cause irreparable harm for which monetary damages alone would be inadequate. Each Party may seek injunctive or other equitable or interim relief from a court of competent jurisdiction in the Controlling Member State or any other Member State with jurisdiction under Regulation (EU) No 1215/2012.

13.2 Without prejudice to any other remedy, where the breach concerns a Trade Secret the Disclosing Party may seek the measures available under national law `[VERIFY national transposition]`:

(a) **Provisional and precautionary measures** under Article 11 of Directive (EU) 2016/943 — including provisional cessation or prohibition of use or disclosure, prohibition of production or marketing of infringing goods, and seizure or delivery-up of suspected infringing goods.

(b) **Corrective measures** under Article 13 — including cessation, recall or destruction of infringing goods, and destruction or delivery-up of documents, materials, or electronic files embodying the Trade Secret.

(c) **Damages** under Article 14 — taking into account, where appropriate, lost profits, unfair profits made by the infringer, and moral prejudice; or, alternatively, a hypothetical royalty.

13.3 Remedies in §13 are cumulative and in addition to any other remedies at law or in equity. Each Party shall preserve the confidentiality of Trade Secrets in any litigation in accordance with Article 9 of Directive (EU) 2016/943 and the procedural rules of the seised court.

## 15. Governing law & jurisdiction

Plain-English summary: governed by the law of a chosen EU/EEA Member State; disputes go to that state's courts under EU jurisdiction rules.

15.1 This Agreement, and any non-contractual obligations arising out of or in connection with it, are governed by the laws of the Controlling Member State (`{{Controlling Member State}}`), excluding its conflict-of-laws rules and the CISG.

15.2 The Parties submit to the exclusive jurisdiction of the courts of `{{Controlling Member State Court}}`, without prejudice to provisional or precautionary measures in any other Member State whose courts have jurisdiction under Article 35 of Regulation (EU) No 1215/2012 ("Brussels I bis").

15.3 This is a business-to-business arrangement; the consumer-residence carve-out in Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 593/2008 ("Rome I") does not apply, and §15.1 governs notwithstanding Article 6.

15.4 Nothing in §15 limits a Party's right to seek interim relief from any court of competent jurisdiction to protect Trade Secrets or other Confidential Information.

## 16. Miscellaneous

Plain-English summary: standard clauses on entire agreement, severability, assignment, counterparts, and electronic signing.

16.1 **Entire agreement.** This Agreement is the entire agreement on its subject matter and supersedes all prior agreements and understandings, oral or written. Neither Party has relied on any representation not expressly set out here.

16.2 **Severability.** If any provision is held invalid or unenforceable, the remainder remains in force, and the invalid provision shall be replaced by an enforceable one closest to the original intent. By virtue of Articles 1(1) and 21 of Directive (EU) 2016/943, the minimum level of protection conferred on Trade Secrets cannot be reduced by contract; any provision that would do so is construed to provide at least that minimum.

16.3 **Amendments.** Effective only if in writing (including signed electronic instruments under eIDAS) and signed by authorised representatives of both Parties.

16.4 **Assignment.** Neither Party may assign without the other's prior written consent, except to an Affiliate or to a successor in a merger, reorganisation, or sale of substantially all relevant assets.

16.5 **No waiver.** Failure or delay in exercising a right is not a waiver.

16.6 **No partnership.** Nothing creates a partnership, joint venture, agency, employment, or fiduciary relationship.

16.7 **Counterparts and electronic signature.** This Agreement may be executed in counterparts, each an original. eIDAS-compliant electronic signatures (advanced or qualified) are valid and binding `[VERIFY national form requirements]`.

16.8 **Language.** Executed in English. Translations are for convenience only; the English version prevails unless mandatory national law of the Controlling Member State requires otherwise.

## 17. Germany (DE-AGB) appendix

Plain-English summary: where this Agreement is used multiple times in Germany or with a German counter-party, German general-terms-control rules and GeschGehG apply alongside the rest of this Agreement; this appendix sets out the German overlays.

17.1 **§§ 305–310 BGB AGB-Kontrolle.** Where this Agreement (or any clause) is "pre-formulated for a multitude of contracts" within § 305(1) BGB and is offered to a counter-party that has not been able to negotiate its content, German general-terms-control law applies: (a) **§ 305c** — surprise clauses do not become part of the Agreement; (b) **§ 307** — clauses unreasonably disadvantaging the counter-party contrary to good faith are invalid, including overly long or indefinite confidentiality obligations imposed on a B2C counter-party; (c) **§ 308 No 1** — restrictive acceptance, assignment, or unilateral-change provisions are scrutinised for reasonableness `[VERIFY whether the relevant clause is individually negotiated, in which case AGB-Kontrolle does not apply]`.

17.2 **GeschGehG (Gesetz zum Schutz von Geschäftsgeheimnissen).** The German Trade Secrets Act transposes Directive (EU) 2016/943. The Parties acknowledge: (a) **§ 2** — Geschäftsgeheimnis definition tracking Article 2(1), including the "angemessene Geheimhaltungsmaßnahmen" requirement; (b) **§ 3** — permitted acquisition, including independent discovery and reverse engineering of lawfully obtained products; (c) **§ 4** — prohibited acts of acquisition, use, and disclosure; (d) **§ 5** — Article 5 carve-outs as transposed; and (e) **§§ 6–17** — civil claims (cessation, destruction, recall, information, damages) and procedural protections.

17.3 **Documentable secrecy steps.** Because § 2(1) GeschGehG requires "angemessene Geheimhaltungsmaßnahmen" the Receiving Party must be able to demonstrate, the Receiving Party shall maintain records of disclosure controls (Representative list, NDAs, technical and organisational measures, incident-response logs) sufficient to evidence those steps in litigation, retained for the Confidentiality Term and the Trade-Secret carve-out period.

17.4 **§ 17 UWG.** The historic trade-secret offence in § 17 UWG has been largely superseded by GeschGehG `[VERIFY current state of UWG residual offences]`; "trade secret" here is construed in accordance with GeschGehG and the Directive.

17.5 **DSGVO Art. 13 / Art. 14.** Where Personal Data is exchanged, each Party shall comply with its controller obligations under Articles 13 and 14 GDPR. Where one Party processes Personal Data on behalf of the other, the Parties shall enter into a separate auftragsverarbeitungsvertrag under Article 28 GDPR.

17.6 **Whistleblower Protection Act (HinSchG).** Germany's Hinweisgeberschutzgesetz (HinSchG) of 2023 transposes Directive (EU) 2019/1937. Nothing here restricts protected reporting to internal channels, external channels, or, where conditions are met, the public; confidentiality obligations do not displace HinSchG protections.

17.7 **Form requirements.** Where German law imposes Schriftform under § 126 BGB, the Parties may satisfy it by qualified electronic signature under § 126a BGB / Article 25(2) eIDAS, subject to mandatory exceptions.

17.8 **Construction.** In case of conflict between §17 and §§1–16, §17 prevails on German-law issues `[VERIFY where Controlling Member State is not Germany]`.
