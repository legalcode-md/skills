## 1. Plain-language summary

This Agreement lets the Parties exchange confidential information for a defined business purpose. Each Party agrees to protect what the other shares, use it only for the Purpose, and not pass it on without permission. Obligations last for a fixed period for ordinary information and as long as the information remains a trade secret under reg. 2 of the Trade Secrets (Enforcement etc.) Regulations 2018 (SI 2018/597). Nothing here stops a Party or its workers from making a protected disclosure, reporting wrongdoing to a regulator, or doing anything required by law.

---

## 2. Parties & purpose

This section identifies who is bound and why. The Parties exchange Confidential Information solely to evaluate and (if appropriate) progress the relationship described in **Annex A** (the **"Purpose"**). Nothing here obliges either Party to enter any further contract. Each Party warrants that its signatory has actual authority to bind it. The Purpose is to be construed narrowly; use outside it — including competitive benchmarking, recruitment of Discloser personnel, or development of competing offerings — is a material breach.

---

## 3. Definitions

This section defines operative terms. Capitalised terms have the meanings below.

- **"Affiliate"** — an entity controlling, controlled by, or under common control with a Party (>50% of voting equity, or equivalent management power).
- **"Confidential Information"** — meaning given in §4.
- **"Discloser"** / **"Recipient"** — the Party (or Affiliate) disclosing / receiving Confidential Information.
- **"Representative"** — a Party's directors, officers, employees, workers, agents, professional advisers, and contractors who need to know Confidential Information for the Purpose.
- **"Trade Secret"** — meaning given in regulation 2 of SI 2018/597: information that (a) is secret in the sense that it is not, as a body or in the precise configuration and assembly of its components, generally known among or readily accessible to persons within the circles that normally deal with the kind of information in question; (b) has commercial value because it is secret; and (c) has been subject to reasonable steps under the circumstances, by the person lawfully in control of the information, to keep it secret. The definition transposes Art. 2(1) of Directive (EU) 2016/943 into post-Brexit retained law and is reproduced under OGL v3.0.
- **"Equitable Confidence"** — the common-law action in breach of confidence (_Coco v. A. N. Clark (Engineers) Ltd_ [1969] RPC 41): (i) information of the necessary quality of confidence; (ii) communicated in circumstances importing an obligation of confidence; (iii) unauthorised use to the detriment of the communicating Party. Contractual rights here are without prejudice to that cause of action.

---

## 4. Confidential Information

This section says what counts as Confidential Information. **Confidential Information** means any information disclosed by or on behalf of the Discloser — in writing, orally, electronically, by inspection of tangible items, or by any other means — that is marked or identified as confidential at disclosure, or that a reasonable person would understand to be confidential given its nature and the circumstances. It includes business plans, customer and supplier lists, pricing, financial information, source code, models, prompts, training data, designs, prototypes, technical specifications, security information, personnel information, and the existence and contents of the discussions.

Confidential Information may include Trade Secrets within regulation 2 of SI 2018/597. The Parties shall take "reasonable steps under the circumstances" to preserve secrecy. Where information is identified as a Trade Secret, the Recipient shall apply the heightened standard of care in §6. Oral or visual disclosures are protected if identified as confidential at disclosure or confirmed in writing within thirty (30) days; failure to confirm does not remove protection where confidentiality is self-evident or where the information satisfies the Trade Secret definition.

---

## 5. Permitted disclosures & use

This section governs how the Recipient may use what it learns. The Recipient may use Confidential Information only for the Purpose, and disclose it only to Representatives who (a) need to know for the Purpose, (b) have been informed of its confidential nature, and (c) are bound by written or professional confidentiality obligations no less protective than those here. The Recipient is responsible for any breach by its Representatives.

The Recipient shall not (i) reverse engineer, disassemble, decompile, or otherwise derive the source form of any object code, model weights, or compiled binary, except as expressly permitted by mandatory law (including ss.50A–50C CDPA 1988); (ii) use Confidential Information to file or prosecute any patent, registered design, or other registrable right; or (iii) use Confidential Information to develop, train, fine-tune, or evaluate any competing product or service.

---

## 6. Standard of care

This section sets the protective standard. The Recipient shall protect Confidential Information using at least the same degree of care it uses for its own confidential information of like importance, and no less than a reasonable standard. Where information is identified as a Trade Secret, the Recipient shall implement the technical and organisational measures necessary to constitute "reasonable steps under the circumstances" within reg. 2(c) SI 2018/597 — including access controls, written confidentiality undertakings from Representatives, secure storage, and prompt notification of any actual or suspected unauthorised access, use, or disclosure.

The Recipient shall comply with the UK GDPR, DPA 2018, and ICO guidance when handling Confidential Information that constitutes personal data. Where the Purpose entails routine processing of personal data, the Parties shall enter a separate Data Processing Agreement compliant with Article 28 UK GDPR.

---

## 7. Exclusions

This section names categories that are not protected. The confidentiality obligations do not apply to information that the Recipient can demonstrate by competent evidence:

- (a) was lawfully in its possession, free of any obligation of confidence, before receipt;
- (b) is or becomes publicly available through no act or omission of the Recipient or its Representatives;
- (c) was lawfully obtained from a third party without breach of any duty of confidence; or
- (d) was independently developed without use of, or reference to, the Discloser's Confidential Information.

In addition:

- (e) Lawful acquisition, use, and disclosure within reg. 9 of SI 2018/597 (corresponding to Art. 3 of Directive 2016/943) — including independent discovery, observation/study/disassembly of a lawfully acquired product not subject to a binding restriction, exercise of worker information and consultation rights, and any other practice consistent with honest commercial practices — is not a breach.
- (f) **Whistleblowing.** Nothing prevents a worker (within ERA 1996) from making a "protected disclosure" under ss.43A–43L ERA 1996 (as inserted by PIDA 1998), nor anything required or protected by law. Any provision purporting to restrict such disclosure is unenforceable to that extent. The Parties acknowledge the Bar Council 2019 guidance and the SRA Warning Notice on NDAs (`{{SRA Warning Notice Version}}`) `[VERIFY]`.
- (g) Reporting to a regulator (FCA, PRA, ICO, HSE, HMRC, CMA) and to a professional body (SRA, BSB) is preserved.
- (h) Reporting to the police, NCA, or any other UK law-enforcement agency, or cooperating with any criminal or regulatory investigation, is preserved.

## 8. Compelled disclosure

This section deals with disclosures the Recipient is forced to make. If required to disclose by a UK court order, statutory notice from the FCA, PRA, ICO, HMRC, SFO, or another competent authority, parliamentary inquiry, or (for a public-body counterparty) a request under FOIA 2000 or the Environmental Information Regulations 2004, the Recipient shall, to the extent legally permitted: (a) promptly notify the Discloser in writing so a protective order may be sought; (b) cooperate, at the Discloser's expense, with reasonable efforts to obtain such relief; and (c) disclose only that portion which counsel advises is legally required, using reasonable efforts to obtain assurances of confidential treatment. Where prior notification is itself prohibited (e.g., a tipping-off restriction), the Recipient is excused from (a) and shall notify the Discloser as soon as the prohibition no longer applies.

## 9. Term & duration of obligations

This section sets time limits. This Agreement takes effect on the Effective Date and continues until the end of the Disclosure Period; confidentiality obligations survive as below.

- **Disclosure Period.** {{Disclosure Period}} (default: two (2) years). After it ends, neither Party need receive further Confidential Information, but obligations attaching to information already disclosed continue.
- **Confidentiality Term.** Obligations for ordinary Confidential Information continue for {{Confidentiality Term}} (default: five (5) years) from disclosure.
- **Trade-Secret carve-out.** Obligations for Trade Secrets continue for {{Trade-Secret Term}} (default: for so long as the information remains a Trade Secret within regulation 2 of SI 2018/597).

The Parties acknowledge that an indefinite contractual obligation of confidence applied to ordinary (non-Trade-Secret) information held by employees post-employment may be unenforceable as an unreasonable restraint of trade (_Faccenda Chicken Ltd v. Fowler_ [1986] ICR 297). The trade-secret carve-out tracks the _Faccenda_ distinction and is not intended to bind any individual employee personally beyond what English common law permits.

## 10. Return or destruction of materials

This section explains what happens to materials when discussions end. Within {{Return Window}} (default: thirty (30) days) after the earlier of the Discloser's written request, termination, or conclusion of the Purpose, the Recipient shall, at the Discloser's election, return or destroy all Confidential Information (including copies and derivative materials) and certify destruction in writing.

The Recipient may retain (i) one archival copy held by its legal function or external counsel solely to evidence compliance; (ii) Confidential Information on routine backups not readily accessible in the ordinary course, securely destroyed per the Recipient's standard schedule; and (iii) information whose retention is required by law, regulator, or court order. Retained information remains subject to this Agreement.

---

## 11. No license; ownership

This section confirms that disclosure does not transfer rights. All Confidential Information remains the Discloser's property. No licence — express, implied, by estoppel, or otherwise — is granted under any patent, copyright, design right, database right, trade mark, Trade Secret, or other IP right, except the limited right to use Confidential Information for the Purpose during the term. Each Party retains all right, title, and interest in any improvements it independently creates without reference to the other's Confidential Information.

## 12. Residuals (OPTIONAL)

**OPTIONAL — only if both Parties expressly agree to a residuals concept.** A residuals clause should be considered carefully under English law because, depending on its breadth, it may dilute trade-secret protections in §4 and §13 and may sit uneasily with the "reasonable steps" requirement in regulation 2(c) of SI 2018/597.

Subject to the Discloser's rights in any patent, copyright, registered design, or Trade Secret, the Recipient's Representatives may use, in the ordinary course of their work, general knowledge, skills, and experience retained in unaided memory, provided that (a) no Confidential Information is intentionally memorised to avoid this Agreement, (b) no Trade Secret is used or disclosed, and (c) the Recipient does not identify the Discloser as the source. This clause does not grant any licence under the Discloser's registered IP rights.

## 13. Remedies & equitable relief

This section explains consequences of breach. A breach or threatened breach may cause irreparable harm for which damages alone may not be adequate. The Discloser is entitled, in addition to other remedies, to interim and permanent injunctive relief, specific performance, and an account of profits, without proving actual damage. Available remedies include:

- (a) provisional and precautionary measures under regs. 13–15 SI 2018/597 (interim injunctions, seizure/delivery up of suspected infringing goods, preservation of evidence);
- (b) corrective measures under reg. 16 (final injunctions, production/marketing prohibitions, recall, destruction of documents or materials embodying the Trade Secret, delivery up);
- (c) damages under reg. 17, taking into account all appropriate factors including actual prejudice (lost profits), unfair profits, and where appropriate moral prejudice; or a lump sum based on at least the royalties that would have been due had authorisation been requested;
- (d) an account of profits in Equitable Confidence; and
- (e) a "springboard" injunction restraining unfair head start (_Terrapin v. Builders' Supply_ [1967] RPC 375).

No liability cap applies to confidentiality obligations unless expressly agreed in writing.

## 14. Notices

This section describes how to send formal notices. Any notice must be in writing in English and is deemed delivered:

- (a) **By hand** — on delivery to the registered office during business hours on a business day;
- (b) **By recorded post** — two (2) business days after first-class recorded delivery within the UK (seven (7) for international), to the registered office;
- (c) **By email** — on transmission to {{Party A Notice Email}} or {{Party B Notice Email}}, with delivery confirmation and provided the email is not bounced; outside business hours, deemed delivered at the start of the next business day.

Email is permitted under the Electronic Communications Act 2000, and electronic signatures applied under retained eIDAS (the Electronic Identification and Trust Services for Electronic Transactions Regulations 2016 and subsequent UK SIs) `[VERIFY retention status]` are valid for execution. Either Party may change its notice address or email by notice given under this section.

## 16. Miscellaneous

This section collects standard boilerplate.

- **Entire agreement.** This Agreement (with its Annexes) is the entire agreement on its subject matter and supersedes all prior discussions. Each Party waives any claim for innocent or negligent misrepresentation; nothing limits liability for fraud.
- **Severability.** Any invalid provision shall be deemed modified to the minimum extent necessary, or deleted; the remainder continues in force.
- **No waiver.** No failure or delay to exercise any right is a waiver.
- **No assignment.** Neither Party may assign without the other's prior written consent, save assignment to an Affiliate or successor in a merger or sale of substantially all of the relevant business.
- **No partnership or agency.** Nothing here creates a partnership, joint venture, agency, or employment relationship.
- **No restraint of trade.** Nothing is intended or shall be construed as a covenant in restraint of trade, non-compete, or non-solicitation. The Parties acknowledge the _Faccenda Chicken Ltd v. Fowler_ [1986] ICR 297 distinction between Trade Secrets (protectable post-employment) and other confidential information acquired in employment (generally not so protectable absent an express and reasonable post-termination covenant).
- **Equitable confidence preserved.** Rights here are without prejudice to, and cumulative with, any cause of action in Equitable Confidence.
- **Counterparts and e-signature.** Executed in counterparts. Signatures via a qualified or advanced electronic signature within retained eIDAS, or an established e-signature platform with audit trail, are valid and binding.
- **Third-party rights.** No non-Party has rights under the Contracts (Rights of Third Parties) Act 1999, save that an Affiliate of a disclosing Party may enforce confidentiality and use obligations directly.
- **OPTIONAL — only if either Party is a public-sector body.** {{Public Sector Party}} acknowledges its public-sector equality duty under s.149 Equality Act 2010 and shall have due regard to it in performing this Agreement.
- **OPTIONAL — only if either Party has annual turnover at or above the MSA threshold.** Each Party with turnover at or above {{Modern Slavery Threshold}} `[VERIFY]` confirms it publishes an annual statement under s.54 Modern Slavery Act 2015 consistent with this Agreement.

> **NDA Whistleblowing Notice.** Nothing in this Agreement prevents either Party or its Representatives from making a protected disclosure under the Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998 / Employment Rights Act 1996 ss.43A–43L, reporting wrongdoing to a regulator or law-enforcement authority, raising a complaint to a professional body, or doing anything required by law. Any clause that purports to do so is, to that extent, unenforceable.

This notice is intended to be substantively equivalent to the SRA Warning Notice on non-disclosure agreements (`{{SRA Warning Notice Version}}`) `[VERIFY]` and reflects Bar Council and Law Commission guidance.
